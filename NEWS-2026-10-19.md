packs, the literature, the related projects and the release feeds. **Reading only.** Nothing was
downloaded, installed, built or run; no engine was started; no inference and no benchmark were

What in it touches us, and what does not:

- **Our card's table is in it, and the notes never mention it.** §1.6. The release notes name exactly

- **Linux in-place GGUF reading (zero-copy page-cached experts, I/O prefetch behind a gate):** 12 GB

### 3b. `661ff29` and `STRATA_ADAPT_OVERLAP` - the mechanism, and why it points the other way for us

From **#2936**'s resolution (2026-10-19T14:12Z): the function changed from

```cpp
// 0.1.43
return !(e && e[0] == '0');   // Linux CUDA: ON by default; =0 turns it off
// 661ff29 (0.1.44.x)
return e && e[0] == '1';      // opt-in on every backend; =1 turns it on
```

and the discriminator is the engine's own startup line: the 0.1.43 build prints the *on* line, the
661ff29 build prints `adaptive overlap: off (STRATA_ADAPT_OVERLAP=1 turns it on)` **with no reason**,
which the reporter reads as "every guard passed, so it is simply the new default". Their rig gains
because a pinned expert arena on **PCIe 3.0 ×16** swaps experts every decode window and overlap hides
that wait; upstream's own numbers in the thread are A4000 **+5..8 %** and P100 **+4.4 %**, while the
flip was made for Blackwell desktops that **lose 19-21 %** with it on. The reporter also states that at
tag `v0.1.45` both the function body and the guard chain are **byte-identical to v0.1.44.1**, so 0.1.45
defaults overlap to off too.

Two lab-relevant corrections come out of the same thread:

- **A 22.6 % arm movement from host placement.** ESXi NUMA pinning (`numa.nodeAffinity`,
  `numa.vcpu.preferHT`) took the dual-ON arm from 78.6 → 96.4 tok/s narrative. Verified effective with
  `esxtop`, not assumed. That is bigger than most effects this lab has queued, and it is the same class
  as the 2026-10-18 retraction (a second engine reloaded mid-bench): **the bench host is part of the
  instrument**.
- **The reporter retracted their own attribution.** The -23/-27 % was first pinned on two build
  variables; the same comment withdraws that: "I'd attributed the -23/27 % to `STRATA_Q6K_EXPERTS=ON` /
  `STRATA_MMQ_KQUANTS=ON`. That was wrong."

**What this file will and will not claim:** `661ff29` and the switch are evidence-primary (bisect,
source, log line). Whether *our* card wants overlap on is **unmeasured and contested by architecture**,
and the note that decides it - #1964's 19-21 % Blackwell number - was **not read at source today**.
`status: lead` for our geometry.

### 3c. The 0.1.45 prefill-chunk default broke text on Windows, and it was filed on 10-17

**#2856** (filed 2026-10-17T07:35Z, last updated 2026-10-17T14:25Z) - yesterday's scan listed it as
"unchanged" and did not open it. Read today, it is the strongest text-level report in this family:

- **Windows 11, prebuilt 0.1.45 HIP engine, R9700/gfx1201, 32 GB**: prompts longer than ~512 tokens
  produce output **completely unrelated to the conversation** - the model answers a different question
  ("The answer is A) Paris" to "1+1?"), describes a non-existent image, or says nothing was asked. 53
  tokens fine, 523 tokens broken. **Root cause is the new default prefill chunk** (4096/2048/1024
  instead of 512), and the release note's own hedge - "prompts shorter than the chunk are unchanged" -
  predicts exactly that split. A/B on the same engine at temperature 0: `--prefill auto` broken for any
  prompt above ~512 tokens, **`--prefill 512` 4/4 correct** on 2.8K and 3.3K prompts including
  multi-turn and system-message constraints. With `--prefill 512`, 0.1.45 decodes **~17 % slower than
  0.1.44.1** on that card (126 vs 148 tok/s).
- **A second reporter (#2856, 2026-10-17T13:17Z) measured 0.1.45 on an RTX 5090** by splitting the
  `strata serve` log at the version banner - real traffic, no downgrade: prompt 2K-50K **111.8 → 106.4**
  (−5 %), **>50K 105.7 → 90.0 (−15 %)**, cold prefill unchanged (~4,700-4,800 tok/s both eras),
  expert-cache hit 84.6 → 83.1 %, and **draft acceptance 74.2 → 67.2 % / 71.6 → 67.1 %, uniformly across
  all buckets including short prompts**. A uniform draft-acceptance drop points at the spec/MTP path,
  not at a workload-mix artifact.

**Applies: yes, twice over.** (i) It is a **second** text-changing 0.1.45 default, independent of
#2940's `presence_penalty`, and the cheaper of the two to neutralise (`--prefill 512`). (ii) The draft
acceptance drop is the same quantity **#3166** moves by 40 % (§3f) and the same quantity our own MTP
result is about (§7, research) - three independent measurements on one variable, which is now clearly
the thing to record in any spec-decoding A/B on this engine. `status: evidence-primary` (A/B with a
named flag and a transcript) for the text; **`board-level, one machine`** for the −17 %.

## 7. Research: the index advanced one day, and the first new paper names our own MTP result

**arXiv's index now reaches 2026-10-16** (newest cs.CL submission **2610.23847**, 2026-10-16T17:58:08Z),
against 2026-10-15 yesterday - so one new day of material is queryable and the rest of the batch is the
re-coverage the standing rule forbids. Five items in it bear on instruments this lab runs:

- **2610.23818, "Acceptance Is Not Speed: Length Inflation in Training-Free Lossy Speculative Decoding"**
  (2026-10-16T17:41:19Z). Twelve target-drafter pairs against lossless verification on identical
  prompts, measuring completion length, verifier rounds, wall clock and accuracy. At each rule's most
  relaxed setting, relaxed verification **accepts 1.34× as many tokens per round but produces 1.54×
  longer completions**, and "for rules that reduce verifier rounds, accuracy typically drops
  substantially", with failures traced to **continued reasoning that exhausts the budget needed to
  deliver a final answer**. `status: evidence-primary`.
  - **Why it is today's most useful paper for us:** our 2026-10-08 MTP measurement found exactly this
    shape and had no name for it - **+31.2 % per token (50.3 vs 38.3 effective tok/s) against −7.3 %
    wall, because MTP emitted +21.7 % more tokens, with accuracy unchanged** (219/221/219 vs 220/219/216).
    1.31× accepted-equivalent against 1.54× length inflation is the same pair of numbers in the same
    direction. The difference is that llama.cpp's spec path is lossless by design, so **the mechanism
    may not be the paper's**; what transfers is the metric discipline the paper argues for, which this
    lab already applies by reporting tokens and wall time together.
  - **Where it is immediately actionable:** Strata's `--spec-min-p 0.5` is a *relaxed acceptance
    threshold*, and #2856's draft-acceptance drop and #3166's 40 % are the same variable. If that knob
    is ever moved here, **score answers and completion length, not accepted tokens**.
  - **Second, uncomfortable fit:** "continued reasoning that exhausts the budget needed to deliver a
    final answer" is our **23-of-178 truncation class** from the 2026-10-08 census (`stopReason=length`
    with no tool call), and the same failure the knowledge instrument hits at a 200 s per-request
    budget on 1,024-token answers. The paper gives that class an external description.
- **2610.23787, "DLite: History-free Speculative Decoding for Agentic AI"** (2026-10-16T17:18:54Z):
  under long, continuously evolving agentic context, existing draft models lose **up to 80 %** of
  acceptance length, and keeping their historical KV costs more than the loss; DLite reuses the target's
  contextual hidden states with a lightweight sequential head, reaching 2-6 acceptance length. `status:
  evidence-primary`. **Applies as a variable to record, not a thing to adopt** (it needs training): our
  own MTP note already measured draft acceptance at **~33 % on free-form code against 0.90-0.97 on
  repetitive tool-call turns**, which is this paper's claim from the other end.
- **2610.23254, "Ignored, Not Refused: What Low Prompt-Injection Success Rates Hide in Small Tool-Using
  Agents"** (2026-10-16). Audits 33,000 trajectories from eleven open-weight agents (2-14B) on InjecAgent
  and AgentDojo: **most failed attacks are ignored (76-80 %) rather than refused (17-18 %)**; changing
  **only the system prompt** moves InjecAgent ASR from **0.2 % to 43 %**; and in **21.7 %** of AgentDojo
  episodes the agent **stayed safe but did not complete the task**. `status: evidence-primary`.

  the stall and declined to act" from "the guard never noticed" - and our own 2026-09-09-style "correct

  third outcome (noticed-and-refused vs not-noticed), not only a catch count.
- **2610.22953, "Recognizing Atari and Assessing Sente: Controlled Evaluation Tasks for Language-Based
