The Case for In Situ Resource Utilization as the Primary Driver of Mars Exploration

While the scientific value of remote sensing and sample return missions is undeniable, the ultimate goal of establishing a permanent human foothold on the Red Planet requires a fundamental shift in strategy. I argue that the most critical area of Mars science today is not merely the search for past life or the mapping of geological features, but the development and validation of In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) technologies. Without a robust understanding of how to extract, process, and use local resources, Mars remains an inaccessible destination rather than a viable frontier.

ISRU offers a solution by leveraging the Martian environment itself.
