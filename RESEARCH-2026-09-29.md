# 12 GB VRAM model candidates: research note

Research date: 2026-09-29. Owner: Kevin, machine DARKTEXAS (RTX 5070 12 GB, 32 GB RAM).
These are candidates and proposed experiments, not new benchmark results. No model
was downloaded, loaded, or run while producing this note. All weights and inference
activity are unchanged.

Companion files: queue-20260929-v2/RESUME.md and staged.json (staged queue, not run).
Prior local evidence is unchanged: see RESULTS-2026-09-20.md, PACKING-RESULTS-2026-09-22.md,
A3B-OFFLOAD-RESEARCH-2026-09-24.md, LOCAL-DECISION-FOLLOWUP-2026-09-25.md, and
ai_bench/candidates-20260929-v1/BONSAI-REVIEW.md.

## Fit rule used here

Weight-file size is not residency. Every number below excludes KV cache, compute
buffers, CUDA context, and desktop VRAM use (about 2 GiB observed idle on this box).
Coding runs must leave >=7.5 GiB VRAM free before load, per the standing gate.
File sizes reported by publishers are decimal GB unless a byte count is given.

## Candidates that fit 12 GB

| Model | Revision pinned | Quant | File | Residency note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ling-3.0-tiny | inclusionAI 9a98e35fe1c9ee255f78dd64771c7ae15a799481 | Q6_K | 6.84 GB | 7.89B total, 1.3B active; full GPU residency plus context |
| Ling-3.0-tiny | bartowski ea072726af0d2e8ba325b2f90fc0efa762105a91 | Q4_K_M | 4.92 GB | same model, lower quant |
| Gemma 4 26B-A4B | unsloth c099eb48e663fd284577b04978a94ffccb261841 | UD-IQ2_M | approx 9.97 GB | fits only with 3-bit KV cache from a fork build |
| Ternary Bonsai 2 27B | official PQ2 3907dc1658db1f78a9826bf8d5bcb8dc65db0d466388937af57f2294fae62ec1 | PQ2 | 7,206,168,928 bytes | 32k context already verified locally on 2026-09-20 |
| Qwen3.6-35B-A3B | bartowski GGUF | Q4_K_M | approx 21 GB | needs CPU expert offload; use the measured A3B recipe |

Ling-3.0-tiny detail: 7.9B total, 1.3B active per token, 3:1 KDA plus MLA hybrid
attention, 128 routed experts with 8 active plus 1 shared, MIT license, thinking mode
configurable per request. The GGUF declares architecture bailingmoe3 with 131072
context (128k, not the 256k quoted in some posts). Publisher reports about 8.34 GiB
peak at 8K context with FP8, on other hardware. Local llama.cpp support: bailingmoe3
landed in mainline via PR 26608 (2026-08-17) and src/models/bailingmoe3.cpp exists in
master; the bartowski quants were built with b10472 and the pinned stock build here is
b10883. Some community GGUFs predate the merge and one third-party repo still ships its
own fork, so confirm the architecture loads before spending a session.

Sources: https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/Ling-3.0-tiny ,
https://huggingface.co/bartowski/Ling-3.0-tiny-GGUF ,
https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/26608

Gemma 4 26B-A4B detail: 128 experts with 8 active, apache-2.0, 262144 declared context,
unsloth dynamic quants plus MXFP4_MOE and MTP variants in the same repository. The
published 12 GB recipe uses UD-IQ2_M with a turboquant fork and turbo3 KV cache at
32768 context, reporting about 10.5 GB used and 40-55 tokens/sec on an RTX 3060 under
headless Debian. That is a single low-traffic repository with no independent
replication, and the fork itself is a second experimental variable.

Sources: https://huggingface.co/unsloth/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-GGUF ,
https://github.com/julien9679/Gemma-4-26B-A4B-on-RTX-3060-12GB

## Candidates that do not fit, with the arithmetic

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (2026-08-26, Qwen Community License 1.0): 125B total with 6B active,
plus a 51B n-gram embedding table and a 4B MTP layer, multimodal, 262144 native context,
GDN plus Qwen Sparse Attention. The n-gram table is a deterministic lookup read once per
forward pass, so it can be served from SSD; that is the mechanism behind a 85 GB build
running on a 64 GB Mac at 36 tokens/sec. Even excluding that table, the most aggressive
usable build published so far is 45.8 GB resident, and expert tensors are explicitly not
practical from disk. The smallest published GGUF in the well-known quant family is
70.10 GB (IQ1_S). Against a 12 GB VRAM plus roughly 32 GB RAM budget this cannot run at
usable speed. Mainline llama.cpp support is also still settling: the support request is
open (issue 27741) and the implementation lives in PR 27742 (qwen4exp).

Sources: https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8-flash-next ,
https://huggingface.co/bartowski/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF ,
https://huggingface.co/AtomicChat/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF ,
https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/27741 ,
https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27742

Qwen3.8-27B (2026-08, dense flagship): Q4_K_M is about 15.33 GB on disk and about
17.3 GB total. Above 12 GB. Only Q3 and lower would fit, at a quality cost that would
confound any comparison against the existing qwen3.5:9b and A3B baselines. Source:
https://localmodel.run/model/qwen3.8-27b , https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF

Gemma 4 31B and Gemma 4 26B at Q4_K_M (about 17 GB): above 12 GB without offload.

An unverified community claim of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next running at 65 tokens/sec on 12 GB
VRAM was seen in search results; the primary thread could not be fetched and the claim
contradicts the published file sizes above. Treat it as unverified.

## Engine levers on this hardware (sm_120)

- Native NVFP4 support for Blackwell landed in llama.cpp release b8967 (PR 22196,
  2026-04-29). The stock coding build here is b10883, so it is included. sm_120 has
  documented differences from datacenter Blackwell: see
  https://github.com/lna-lab/blackwell-geforce-nvfp4-gemm
- 3-bit KV cache (turbo3) is required to fit Gemma 4 26B-A4B with a 32k context on a
  12 GB card. It exists only in a fork, so any run using it is an engine experiment,
  not a model-only comparison.
- MTP speculative decoding is trained into Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and Ling-3.0, and Gemma 4
  ships MTP files. No local measurement exists here; treat any speed claim as unverified.
- Ternary path: the Prism runtime release moved from prism-b10709-9a9394a to
  prism-b10743-adfffbe on 2026-09-25 with unchanged official PQ2 weights. See
  candidates-20260929-v1/BONSAI-REVIEW.md for the revision audit.

## Research papers worth tracking

- arXiv 2608.01078: post-training quantization of reasoning LLMs at 1.58 bits.
- alphaXiv 2609.16338: breaking the 1.58-bit barrier for ternary LLMs (about two weeks old).
- BTC-LLM (ACL 2026): sub-1-bit quantization with a learnable binary codebook.

These are research-stage and have no verified Windows/x86 runtime here.

## Local evidence that should not be repeated

- Ternary Bonsai 2 PQ2 at 32k context: loops on visible harness files and times out;
  the isolated-workspace follow-up scored 16/21 and also timed out.
- MiMo Q4: repeated one reasoning sentence 3,043 times in the paths task; more timeout
  and more output budget did not fix it.
- A3B expert offload: speed only, 33.90/32.72 tokens/sec at 32 CPU expert layers and
  8192 context; not a quality result.
- Local decision models: OpenJev preserves 46 synthetic probabilities but fails the 3 s
  warm p95 gate; on 18 archived windows it catches 0/4 stalls with 0/14 false alarms.

## Proposed order of work

1. Ling-3.0-tiny Q6_K: new architecture, mainline support, fits fully on GPU, MIT.
   Highest information gain per GB.
2. Swift Bonsai 2 PQ2: already identified as the variation aimed at the repetition
   problem, with the caveat that its headline reduction is historical and the current
   IFBench mean gain is 1.7 percent.
3. Gemma 4 26B-A4B UD-IQ2_M with turbo3 KV: label it an engine experiment; the fork is
   the treatment, not the model.

All three are staged in queue-20260929-v2 with pinned revisions, unchanged gates, and
no downloads. The frozen candidates-20260929-v1 queue is untouched and still awaits the
ready message plus a passing preflight.

## Source quality

Primary sources were used for every number above: publisher model cards, the Qwen
release blog, Hugging Face API metadata for revisions and declared architectures, and
llama.cpp issues, pull requests, and release tags. Aggregator sites (modelfit,
promptquorum, benchlm, willitrunai, localmodelwatch, smeltcore, llamaperf) appeared in
search results and were read but are not relied on; they publish no reproducible method.
No benchmark number in this note was measured on this machine.
