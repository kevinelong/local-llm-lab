## 1. The headline correction: llama.cpp already had the expert cache

The lab's `STRATA-ALTERNATIVES-2026-10-11.md` concluded that the "2x faster" expert cache was
unobtainable here - *"we cannot get this by bumping our pinned build - there is no upstream flag to
set"* - and routed us to waiting for a re-opened PR, building a fork, or using Strata's cache
instead. The 2026-10-14 scan carried that forward by naming the two unmerged PRs as blocked. **That
is wrong as of today, and the flag merged one day before that first conclusion was written.**

- **PR #29887, "llama : add a GPU cache for MoE experts kept in host memory" (am17an), merged
  2026-10-07T18:07:46Z** - `https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/29887`. It adds
  **`--moe-cache-mib N`** plus `moe_cache_size` in `llama.h`: a GPU cache for the routed experts that
  live in host memory, i.e. exactly the `--cpu-moe` configuration this lab runs. Its own numbers
  (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next Q4_0, temperature 0, small batches of 32 tokens or fewer): RTX 4090 25.0 ->
  39.4 tok/s (1.57x, 72% hit) -> 40.7 (1.62x, 77%); RTX 5090 30.8 -> 54.5 (1.77x) -> 67.8 (2.20x,
  89%). `status: evidence-primary` (merged; the PR is the source of the numbers).
- **PR #30112** (merged 2026-10-08T10:20:50Z) extends the same cache across multiple GPUs; the flag
  is per GPU. Two RTX 4090s, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next Q4_0: `--fit` 34.78 tok/s -> `--fit
  --moe-cache-mib 6544` 60.38 (1.74x, 85.9% hit) -> `-cmoe --moe-cache-mib 15000` 64.39 (1.85x,
  95.8%). `status: evidence-primary`.
- **Our pinned `b11461` does not have it, which is why the 10-11 conclusion looked right**: the
  merge is 2026-10-07 18:07Z and b11461 is the 2026-10-07 build. A 2026-10-09 video (below) puts the
  first release carrying the flag at **b11480**. So the correct statement is "our build predates it
  by one day", not "upstream has no flag".
- **A same-PC comparison now exists** for the card class above ours (second-hand, from the video in
  section 7): RTX 5070 Ti 16 GB + 64 GB DDR4 on a 2-bit FlashNext pack - llama.cpp with plain
  offload 22.4 tok/s, an 8 GB expert cache 49.4 tok/s, Strata 85.8 tok/s; prompt read 249 -> 206
  tok/s against Strata's 2,320. `status: lead` - the author says the fork test is his own, one run.
- **The cache size is not monotonic, and that is the part to carry into a design.** The same video
  collects owner reports on 12 GB cards: one RTX 3060 fell from 32 to 12 tok/s with an 8 GB cache,

  lab's own `--expert-cache 1500` arm on Strata was a regression in the same direction (6-12% on the

  measurement.

## 6. Research and harness methodology

The literature this window splits into two piles: engine mechanisms we cannot use without engine
work, and evaluation methodology we can adopt for free. The second pile is the valuable one.

**Adoptable method, no download:**

- **TRACE** (arXiv **2610.11678**, 2026-10-08, R. Gaonkar/Prime Intellect) - a protocol that mutates
  one part of an evaluation, runs paired runs, and rescores unchanged trajectories to separate a
  capability change from a measurement change. In 25 synthetic tasks, **renaming tools drops a
  scripted agent 0.250 with identical operations**; on tau-squared-bench misleading tool names lower
  reward 0.20-0.44; **identical reruns flip 15-36% of task outcomes**; two frontier judges disagree
  on **57%** of the same records. That last number is the external counterpart of this lab's own
  repeat rule (`ledger2` has swung 62-226 on one model). `status: evidence-primary`.
- **OnTrack** (arXiv **2610.12375**, 2026-10-08) - a streaming monitor that compares an agent's steps
  and dependencies against recorded successful runs at ~**1 ms/step**, degrading from plan-violation
  detection to **loops, stalls and repeated tool calls** as access falls; on SWE-bench trajectories
  its ranking on the first 8 steps beats content-similarity baselines by **+0.057 AUROC**, and an
  abort policy saves ~**18%** of compute with **5 of 6 aborts (83%)** correct. This is the only new
  primary-source work in the window on the stall class this lab has measured as unfindable by
  decision models (0 of 4, 0 of 4). `status: lead`.
- **RedundancyBench** (arXiv **2605.29893**, v2 2026-10-14) - 1,920 trajectories, 3,613 step-level
  annotations, redundant-step detection; hard even for frontier models. A labelled set for a
  step-efficiency rail. `status: evidence-primary`.
- **Strong Harness Narrows the Model Gap** (arXiv **2610.19999**, 2026-10-14) - the weakest model in
  a decomposed pipeline beats the strongest model alone (**0.948 vs 0.806**, p<0.001) on 153 bills.
  A clean harness-versus-model attribution method, which is the argument our census already makes
  from the other direction (45 of 99 sub-perfect sessions were harness or platform artefacts).
- **Evaluating Local LM Agents for Reproducible Data Engineering** (arXiv **2610.11482**,
  2026-10-08) - deterministic checkers score executed scripts, tables, files and figures; a
  workspace-condition change is worth **26.7-52.0 points**, and a quantized **9B reaches 69.3% at
  ~6.5 GB**. Artifact-execution scoring at our memory scale.
- **HPCAgent-Bench** (arXiv **2610.20673**, 2026-10-14) - executing-artifact benchmark with a hidden
  fuzzed-input judge; interventions change speedup significantly in only **2 of 40** setups but
  change token cost by up to **3.0x**. Worth having for the "cost of getting to green" column the
  10-14 scan asked for.
- **TrialPD-Bench** (arXiv **2610.18507**, 2026-10-13) - scores **evidence recall from the agent's
  own trace**, not just the final answer; agents find the evidence and then fail the last mile of
  transcribing it.

**Engine mechanisms, for the record (none cheap here):** SpecFit (arXiv **2610.15417**, 2026-10-11)
chooses the speculative draft subtree by predicted throughput, modelling the per-layer **union of
experts the subtree activates** - on Qwen3-30B-A3B it decodes **21.6% / 7.0% faster than EAGLE-3 at
the same expert cache** (code `Chlience/SpecFit`); that union-cost model is exactly the quantity a
disk-streaming engine has to budget. LMCache block-device characterisation (arXiv **2610.21249**,
2026-10-15): the legacy path looks read-free only while the page cache is warm, then issues **~1 GB
of sequential reads in 683 ms** - and **Strata issue #2734** (2026-10-16) is a request to integrate
LMCache. KV compression: **AlignPred** (2610.17970, 2026-10-13) cuts persistent KV storage 42-72%
with CPU decoding; **E8 lattice key quantisation** (2610.15384, 2026-10-11) gets ~6x KV compression
with decode parity; **MLAfication** (2610.17202, 2026-10-12) converts Qwen3-30B-A3B-class MoE models
to MLA for 94.73% KV reduction at 4-bit KV; **ChampionKV** (2610.18026, 2026-10-13) allocates KV
budget head-wise and helps most on 70B under tight budgets. All of these need engine work; they are
leads for whichever engine gains a KV knob next.

**Projects.** `ahwurm/localharness` moved three times - v0.17.2 (2026-10-09), **v0.18.0
(2026-10-12)**, v0.18.1 (2026-10-15) - and v0.18.0 adds two mechanisms worth reading:
**"stuck detection counts progress"** (a repeated call whose trusted result keeps changing is
progress until a call-only ceiling, `stuck_detector.call_only_multiplier` default 3, that escalates
regardless of the result - their false-alarm control for polling loops, where our rule is
identical-outcome repetition at 4/4 caught and 1/14 false alarms); and **window autosense**, which
prints served/trained/fits and sizes `-c` and `--cache-ram` from the GGUF header. Its own caveat: a

evidence-primary`. Everything else the 10-14 scan named is unchanged: `zjunlp/OneDayAgent` has one

## 8. What this adds to the plan, in the order I would do it

1. **Read our Strata config for the KV flags, then one KV-residency A/B at fixed capacity.**
   *Instrument:* the engine's own per-request and cold-prompt rates plus the ledger score - exactly
   what settled the 0.1.41 A/B on 2026-10-08. *Cost:* a config read (free, no engine) then two arms
   of the existing 64k ledger run, three roots each (~4 min per root). *Result that counts:* the
   cold-prompt rate and the expert-cache occupancy move outside the box's own repeat spread, or they
   do not. **Cheapest real win in this scan** - no download, no new engine, and it is the one knob
   family our own arm-B regression already implicated. *Gated on:* a declared change if it touches a
   pinned config.
2. **Bump a copy of llama.cpp past b11480 and ladder `--moe-cache-mib`.**
   *Instrument:* the 414-check ranking metric plus the ledger's decode and cold-prompt rates against
   the frozen b11461 baseline. *Cost:* one engine download (a decision, not a step), then three or
   four cache sizes x three roots. *Result that counts:* the reference config's ledger rate moves -
   and the **ladder is required, not optional**, because both the upstream reports and the video's
   12 GB owner reports say the effect is non-monotonic, so "one setting" would be the wrong design.
   This is also the scan's own correction: the 10-14 baseline said this was unobtainable.
3. **Adopt a noise floor per workload, and apply it to every tune from here on.**
   *Instrument:* n >= 3 same-settings roots per arm and a sign test over per-task pairs, which is
   the method Profile Studio uses (6 of 6 turns, p = 0.031) and the method the 2026-10-15 video
   arrived at the hard way. *Cost:* zero downloads; it is a reporting rule. *Result that counts:* a
   stated minimum detectable effect for a config change on this box, and at least one previously
   "won" tuning claim re-examined against it. Two independent sources in one window is enough to
   stop judging tunes by eye.
4. **Seed the expert cache from real prompt history at fixed capacity.**
   *Instrument:* `ledger2` (the battery that actually discriminates), with hit rate alongside.
   *Cost:* no download; one arm pair on the engine we have. *Result that counts:* hit rate and
   `ledger2` move, or the hot set is not the lever we think it is. The evidence for trying it comes
   from three places now: llama.cpp discussion #27149's 6% random versus 42% calibrated hit rate,
   Colibri issue #2109 asking for a warm start, and our own arm-B/C results that showed capacity
   alone is not enough.
5. **Take the decision track onto Ollama's new native path.**
   *Instrument:* the 18-window decision screen and `laya_replay.py` (already parameterised by
   `--engine`/`--checkpoint`), which last ran 2026-10-15 with 0 of 4 stalls caught and 14 of 18
   correct at the frozen 0.85 threshold. *Cost:* an Ollama upgrade decision (0.40.1 -> 0.40.4 or
   0.40.5) plus one replay - minutes, not hours. *Result that counts:* the same model on the new
   path reproduces the frozen fields, or the native path changes the probabilities. Worth doing
   because the last replay's latency finding was refuted by ~10x and the engine path is now the
   variable.
6. **Score the Coder IQ1_M pack at n >= 3 against #2763's 12 GB figures.**
   *Instrument:* the ledger, on the pack we already have staged, against the community line of 20.5
   tok/s chat / ~20 tok/s agent turns at 53-63% cache hit on a 12 GB card with 32 GB of RAM.
   *Cost:* ~4 min per root; the pack is on disk. *Result that counts:* our 64 GB box clears their
   rate, and our bimodal `7,70,7,64,70` record either resolves into a rate or is confirmed as
   bimodality rather than noise.
7. **Put the reference model on the second engine.**
   *Instrument:* the nine-battery suite and the 414-check metric, on Strata at 65,536 context.
   *Cost:* a model download plus a repack plus a sweep (1,300-2,700 s) - the largest item here.
   *Result that counts:* the same model scores the same on both engines or it does not, which makes
   the engines comparable for the first time. *Gated on:* Strata issue #2747's Qwen3.6 OOM report -
   the route is new and already has one failure report, so the cost estimate is a lower bound.

Not on the list, deliberately: the stall-detection mechanisms in section 6 (OnTrack, localharness's
call-only ceiling, RedundancyBench) are real and named, but that class has an existing owner - the
four failure modes and the transcript rule - and these arrive as papers and third-party code rather
than as a switch. They are worth a design read the next time the stall rule is touched, not a queue
item now.
