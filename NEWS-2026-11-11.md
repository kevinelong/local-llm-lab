## 3. [GH][VRAM] Strata: 100 items, and four of them change how a row must be declared

### 3.1 `#3354` - the model-card penalty is why `drafts accepted 0 of 0` happens, and it is not only qwen35moe

Open since **2026-10-20**, now **8 comments**, last at **2026-11-11T08:35Z**. This is the thread that explains the
qwen36 MTP failure we recorded on 2026-10-21 (`#3508`) and the `presence_penalty: 1.5` defect we recorded on 2026-10-18
(`#2940`), and the two turn out to be the same bug.

The mechanism, from the reporter's line references: `serve/server.py`'s `MODEL_CARD_SAMPLING["qwen35moe"]` is
`{"thinking": {"temperature": 1.0, "top_p": 0.95, "top_k": 20, "presence_penalty": 1.5}, ...}`; the q36 engine's
door is `const bool spec = E.mtp_d > 0 && !smp.needs_logits() && !adm;` with
`needs_logits() = temperature > 0 || repeat != 1 || freq != 0 || present != 0`. **A request that names no sampling
arrives with a presence penalty, so `spec` is false and MTP never drafts** - while the same request straight to the
engine (`GEN 64 <ids>`) drafts *"43 of 44"* and reads ~150 tok/s against 73-88. The engine's `--mtp 2` loads and warms
up, which is why the log looks healthy.

Two later comments widen it beyond qwen35moe, and the third confirms them:

- **2026-11-07T16:02Z**, the author summarising a P40 report: *"the model-card default also reaches the generic
  Flash-Next path. There it doesn't switch drafting off, but a `temperature 0` request that doesn't name the penalties
  still runs with `presence_penalty 1.5`, so its output isn't greedy. A client that wants greedy output from Strata
  0.2.x has to send `presence_penalty 0`, `frequency_penalty 0` and `repetition_penalty 1` explicitly; leaving them out
  doesn't mean 'off'."*
- **2026-11-08T21:36Z**, a third party on **4x RTX 3090, Flash-Next UD-IQ4_XS, v0.2.12 (`146df3c4`), 16 slots, 262K,
  `--kv int8`, `--spec 4`, `--prefill 512`**: the penalty reaches `temperature: 0` requests *"on stock v0.2.12"*,
  costing *"little speed in our runs"* but *"a lot more on decode paths that go serial when a request is penalised, as
  your `--pipeline-windows` branch shows"* - the `--pipeline-windows`-is-switched-off consequence we recorded on
  2026-10-18.
- **2026-11-11T08:35Z**, the author, confirming the second: *"It confirms the card's `presence_penalty 1.5` reaches
  `temperature: 0` requests on Flash-Next too, on hardware this issue didn't cover ... Drafting being switched off is
  specific to qwen35moe: the q36 engine drafts only when a request is strictly greedy (`needs_logits()` is false, and a
  presence penalty makes it true). The Flash-Next decode path doesn't have that check, so there the penalty costs only
  a few percent on stock."*

**Applies to every Strata row we hold, and it is a second, independent reason beside `#5626`** (`NEWS-2026-11-10`
section 2). The 2026-10-20 q1 pair read **letters 92/93 in all four arms**; its pack was `q2_0` (Flash-Next), so by
today's reading those four arms ran **with `presence_penalty 1.5` attached to `temperature 0` requests** - i.e. they
compared *penalised* greedy output, which is why the letters matched across arms (the penalty was constant) while no arm
was strictly greedy. It also bounds what the q1 row can be quoted as: a KV-residency effect on **penalised** decoding.
The 2026-10-22 note that *"MTP does fire on Windows on this path"* (11 drafts offered, 9 accepted, on the same `q2_0`
pack) is consistent - Flash-Next has no `needs_logits()` gate, so the penalty costs it a few percent instead of switching
drafting off.

**Instrument:** the declaration block. **Cost:** an edit - declare `presence_penalty`/`frequency_penalty`/
`temperature` on every Strata arm, and one start reading `drafts accepted` with and without explicit zeros.

## 8. [ARX] The index advanced to Wednesday 11 November, and the best paper prices our admission gate

### 8.1 `2611.13412` - Bursts That Outlast Themselves: prefix-cached agent serving is metastable (2026-11-10, cs.DC)

The best item of the window, because it measures the failure our gate is built to prevent and finds that **the remedy is
not the one we use**.

- *"a hit costs an incremental prefill of about a thousand tokens, a miss recomputes a 10-40K-token context. We show that
  this makes prefix-cached agent serving metastable. With an identical base workload, a few minutes of extra arrivals
  leaves a vLLM server in a degraded regime - a **~10 % hit ratio, about half the throughput, and a backlog that grows
  for the remaining 70 minutes** - while its no-burst twin stays healthy (**9 of 12 pairs; none in the reverse
  direction**)."*
- It is not a vLLM artifact: the regime *"also appears with Qwen3-32B on SWE-bench agent trace shapes and on SGLang,
  which tolerates larger triggers"*, and it is **absent without prefix caching**.
- The mechanism is a positive feedback loop: *"Slower service keeps more sessions in the system, their working set
  outgrows the KV pool, and every turn becomes a recomputation; a first-order workload-balance condition, calibrated
  independently, brackets when the regime can drain."*
- **The two recovery levers, measured:** *"throttling new admissions recovered **1 of 7** matched collapsed runs, while
  suspending admitted sessions against a capacity-based limit recovered **all of them** within minutes. A **CPU offload
  tier delays the collapse but does not prevent it**. A budgeter that bounds the resident KV demand of active sessions to
  the pool, with thresholds that need no per-workload tuning, prevented the failure in every run we tested."*

**Applies, and it is the cleanest statement of a defect we have measured without naming:** our admission gate is
*"throttle"* - it reads free commit and free VRAM **before** a start and cannot hold them, and on 2026-10-31 four of
twelve arms were destroyed by the user's renders *after* admission. The paper's answer is not a better start-time check
but a **capacity-based bound on resident state, enforced during the run**. `[INFERENCE]` on our box the resident state is
the expert cache plus the parked conversations, and the "suspend" lever is Strata's parked-conversation eviction
(`#5697`, `#2934`) - the thing that already exists and that our arms never measured as an admission control.

**Instrument:** two arms, identical prompt, one with a 4 GiB page-touching process present from the start, one joining
mid-run; read `cache_n`/`reused` and tok/s before and after. **Cost:** ~15 min, no download. This **subsumes** the
contention item `NEWS-2026-11-10` section 12.7 and gives it a hypothesis.

### 8.2 `2611.14246` - SinkState: the recurrent state in a hybrid model is low-rank, and serving it dense costs 1.9x the memory (2026-11-10, cs.LG)

*"Hybrid models combine softmax attention with linear attention, which compresses history into fixed-size recurrent
states ... the queries, keys, and values of linear layers concentrate in a stable, low-dimensional subspace, a phenomenon
we call the **linear attention sink**, so each state is effectively low-rank, with a rank that varies across states. Yet
existing serving systems keep these redundant states dense, reading and rewriting them in full at every decoding step, so
their memory traffic grows with the batch size and dominates the decoding cost."*

Measured on **four hybrid models with Mamba-2 and Gated DeltaNet (GDN) layers, 4B to 120B**: peak recurrent-state memory
**-2.6x (-1.9x average)** and decoding **1.58x (1.25x average)** against Mamba-SSM and FLA, *"while maintaining
accuracy"*.

**Applies:** GDN is the layer family llama.cpp's `#32078` (section 1.3) just simplified and Qwen3.8's hybrid attention
uses. The paper's claim - **the state is low-rank and we store it dense** - is the mechanism behind every hybrid-KV
accounting dispute we have recorded (Ollama's 4-8x over-prediction, Strata's fp32 q36 KV, `#19632`'s block counting). It
is a serving-system change, not a flag: nothing on this box ships it, so the item is a *prediction* - a KV accounting
that assumes dense state over-estimates, and one that assumes recurrence under-estimates.

### 8.3 `2611.13017` - Multi-Future Drafting: product-ranked verify trees are 27-33 % chimeras (2026-11-09, cs.AI)

This one names **our drafter**. *"Block drafters such as **DFlash** draft a block of future tokens in one forward pass,
and draft trees such as DDTree verify the prefixes ranked highest by the product of the drafter's per-position
distributions ... we show that a product misallocates it: when the continuation can follow several futures, it splices
them into **chimeras**, prefixes stitched together from different futures that the target never produces, and gives them
as much budget as the futures themselves. Barring ties, no product-ranked tree can hold two futures that disagree in m
positions without **2^(m-1)-1 chimeras**, however well the drafter is trained, and in practice **chimeras make up 27-33 %
of a 64-node tree**."* Their fix restores coupling with a latent variable (**K learned mode embeddings**) and a mixture
tree, *"losslessly and with K+1 extra vectors"*: on four Qwen targets 4B-27B at batch 1, *"up to **7.8x** speedup,
5-8 % faster than the best single-mode tree trained identically and, on Qwen3-4B/8B, **9-16 % faster than the released
DARTree**"*.

**Applies as an explanation, not a lever:** DFlash2 is the default drafter on sm_120 (`NEWS-2026-11-04`), and our
acceptance readings (0.62-0.84, and the 98 % saturation warning) are properties of a product-ranked tree. The paper says
a third of a 64-node tree is budget spent on sequences the target would never emit - the mechanism behind TraceSpec's
*"aggregate acceptance can rise with context length while declining within matched late browsing actions"*
(`2610.41177`, `NEWS-2026-11-02`), i.e. acceptance is a property of the *tree*, not only the drafter. Its own fix needs
a retrained drafter, so the local test is not "splice this in"; it is "**report the tree size beside acceptance**",
because a 27-33 % waste is a function of it.
