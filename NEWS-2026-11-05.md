Verbatim: "a bare `--prefill auto` now reads prompts in chunks of up to **32768** tokens (it was 8192) on cards of compute

itself and speed did not change (one 12 GB card **4,608** tokens, two 12 GB cards **7,168** tokens)." (b) Under Known

`[INFERENCE]` this also means the 0.1.57 *engine* would read our 19,769-token ladder prompt at ~7,936 rather than the
2,048 we declared, only if our declaration stopped being honoured - the clamp line remains the check, and our gate
already treats a granted chunk that differs from the declared one as VOID (`chunk_ladder.py` rule (a)).

### 1.4 the near-tie mechanism, stated by the vendor, with the sentence that retires some of its own KL figures

Under **Known problems**, first item: "**The first answer of some agent prompts depends on the chunk size.**" The
mechanism, verbatim: "The sparse block selection inside the model's attention sits on near-ties (the gap between the
lowest chosen and the highest unchosen block is under 1e-3 on **75 to 93 %** of queries), so any change in rounding flips
which blocks are picked on **70 to 90 %** of queries, and a few last positions amplify it. Changing the chunk size in
either direction (4096, 6144, 12288, 16384, 32768 against 8192) moves a few agent prompts by an **O(1) KL**; plain text
barely moves (**KL about 1e-5**). It is not specific to 32768." On the 96-request fair set (the suite's own prompts and
later-turn agent contexts, RTX PRO 5000, IQ3_S, all experts resident): 32768 against 8192 **mean KL 0.023, p90 0.029,
max 1.24**.

Then the sentence that matters for this lab's bookkeeping: "**Earlier agent-prompt KL figures (the route-tail skip 0.203,
the K-quant kernels 0.065 and 0.052) may be inflated by this effect and were not re-measured.**"

Three consequences, in order of cost:

1. **A KL row without `chunk=` is unreadable** for agent-shaped prompts. Our own declarations carry KV format, context,
   resident budget and reserve; they do not carry the chunk on the KL side even where they carry it on the prefill side.
2. **"Same flags, same answer" is a claim about plain text only.** We already knew near-tie *words* flip at temperature 0
   (`NEWS-2026-10-25`, the 0.2.5 note); this extends it from sampling ties to **attention block selection**, at a much
   higher flip rate, and attributes it to *our* model family's sparse attention.
3. **The vendor's own quality rows are now self-declared as possibly inflated** on the agent tasks, which is the same
   discipline our `lab-evidence-discipline` skill asks of us, arriving from the other direction.

### 1.5 #5059 is closed: the false failure is fixed, the resident budget is not mentioned

**#5059** - the 64 GB / 16 GB VRAM Windows item our last three scans carried as the bump's **prerequisite** - **closed
2026-11-05T13:20:25Z** after 8 comments. The fix is in the 0.2.11 body as "**#5177, #5059: the app declared a start failed
while the engine finished loading fine** (Control's owner process answered its status request only after planning, with a
5 s limit; the first-token check shared the load's deadline). Planning now runs in a thread with phases (preparing,
launching, loading, checking the engine, verifying generation), the status call is retried while the owner lives, and the
**first-token check has its own 180 s budget**."

Read precisely, that is the *symptom* (the app not accepting a started engine) and not the *cause* the reporter
identified (0.2.10 no longer writes `--resident-budget-gib`, so the engine takes the 20 s arena wait, the `mmap`
fallback and a ~43 GiB page-locked copy). **The body nowhere says 0.2.11 restores a resident budget of its own**, and the
one place `resident-budget-gib` appears in the whole release is a *test configuration* in Known problems (sec. 1.7).
Consequence for the bump, unchanged from `NEWS-2026-11-04` sec. 1.3: **declare `resident_budget_gib` explicitly in the run
config**; do not rely on the app's default, in either direction.

One further line, from `#3750`'s entry, is a declaration fact about every Strata row this lab has ever taken: "**The
engine still skips every memory adaptation when `--vram-reserve-mib` is given and the app always passes it; that part needs
an engine change and is not in this release.**" Our ladder declarations all pass `--reserve 900` / `--reserve 2000`, so
our arms ran with the engine's own memory adaptation switched off by construction. That does not invalidate them - it was
constant across arms - but it means a Strata row's reserve is not a *tuning hint* the engine can override, and any future
arm that omits it is not comparable.

### 1.7 the memory-shaped defect 0.2.11 ships with, on a 64 GB PC

### 1.8 #5213 now has two vendors' cards, and it is a one-start check on ours

The original report (`NEWS-2026-11-04` sec. 1.6) was a 32 GB R9700: Auto picks `--draft-conf 0.35` where the engine's
**8,312 slots** prescribe **0.5**; measured **66.7 -> 71.1 tok/s** and **58.4 % -> 67.0 %** acceptance. The new comment
(`tskorupka-emiteo`, 2026-11-05T11:39:45Z) is a **single RX 7900 XTX 24 GB on HIP**, engine 0.1.56,
`--max-context 262144 --kv int8 --kv-resident 32768`, Auto/DFlash2:

| `--draft-conf` | decode tok/s | note |
|---|---|---|
| **0.7** (what the start picks) | 85.3 | the 3,000-7,499 step |
| **0.5** (what its **10,712** slots prescribe) | **94.5** | **+11 % over the automatic value** |
| 0.35 | 78.9 | "clearly worse" |

Cause, unchanged and unpatched: `strata_control/drafting.py:505` `expected_slots()` does not read `--kv-resident`, so it
prices the KV session **1,590 MiB too high** and lands **815 slots** off. The new comment adds the operational half: "The
only way I found to keep 0.5 across starts was `user_args` in the run config with the server started directly (**see
#5178**), because `strata launch` regenerates the config." **0.2.11 fixed the 0.35-vs-0.8 launch-file bug (#5178, #5132,
#4985) but not this one** - #5213 is still open, updated 2026-11-05T11:39:45Z, and the release's Known problems still
carries "`--set draft-conf=off` with DFlash2 is replaced by the automatic value (0.35) with no notice".

Why ours by construction: our declarations put `--kv-resident` below `--max-context` with no explicit `--draft-conf`. The
misprice inflates the estimated slot count, therefore picks a **lower** step than the engine's actual slots warrant. Our
granted counts from the resident ladder are ~1,500-3,000 slots, i.e. a different band from either reporter's, so the same
bug would show as a different wrong value - which is why the check is "read both lines and compare to `CONF_STEPS`", not
"expect 0.5".

## 3. [GH][VRAM] Adjacent projects, memory-shaped only

### 3.1 QEngine #8: the same model, the same symptom, and a third engine's explanation

**`bpx03/QEngine` #8** (created 2026-11-03T20:36:16Z, updated 2026-11-05T13:34:19Z, open, 3 comments) - "Flash-Next: after
several conversations, a single stream decodes on about half the expert cache (idle conversations are reclaimed only in
batched steps)". Reproduced on 1.3.4.3, "on a rig with plenty of host RAM, so memory pressure isn't the cause". Each run:
fresh boot, single-stream bench (1,000-token replies), then 5 separate conversations of 5 turns each, then single-stream
benches until two agree within 5 %:

| `FLASH_MAX_SEQS` | narrative decode, fresh -> settled | decode at 10K depth | prefill 10K |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8** | 166.8 -> 122.5 -> 132.5 -> **134.0 (-20 %)** | 102.9 -> **74.2** | flat |
| **1** | 174.6 -> 170.9 -> **168.3 (-4 %, within the run's CV)** | 100.7 -> 92.3 | flat |

The engine's own expert-cache line explains it: with 8 slots, a fresh bench spread over slots 1-7 holds **8,394-8,862 of
9,070** slots with 300-330 misses/step, while every bench after the five conversations (all on slot 0) holds **3,766,
then 4,962 of 9,070** with **459-892** misses/step; with 1 slot the whole run stays at **8,902-8,954**. Cause as source
reading: `reclaim_idle()` (`FLASH_EC_RECLAIM_S`, 30 s) is called from one place, `qe_step_batch()` after `resume_paused`,
"the single-sequence path never reaches it, so a lone stream never gets the expert cache's room back". `FLASH_KV=ram`
avoids it because idle conversations then hold no VRAM (a 6-seat 4x RTX 5080 run saw no drop).

**What this changes for us, despite the different engine.** It names a **covariate our ladders never recorded**: how many
conversations and requests the engine has served *earlier in the same process*. Our 2026-10-20 rule says record what ran
before a measurement, and our #4826 lesson says an untimed warm-up is mandatory or the cache manufactures a gain; this is
the mirror image - **prior conversations can shrink the cache and manufacture a loss**, and it persists across subsequent
benches ("and stays there"). Our configuration is `--parallel 1`, which this report says does *not* trigger it - so the
finding is a scoping fact for our rows (one slot protects us) and a hazard for any future multi-slot arm.

### 3.2 ik_llama.cpp #2849: "minimise host bytes per token", not "maximise resident VRAM"

**`ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp` #2849** (created and updated 2026-11-05T14:34:37Z, open, 0 comments) - the reporter says it is
"a llama.cpp-style engine of ours (not mainline, not a fork of this repo)" and asks whether the observations match.
Setup: one A40 48 GB, 1 TB host RAM across 2 NUMA nodes, PCIe 4.0 x16; MoE with **~100 GB of expert weights, top-8 of
288, 45 MoE layers**, experts IQ2_XS (gate/up) + IQ3_XXS (down).

- **Bandwidth ceiling:** pinned H2D **25.3 GB/s at 1 and at 8 streams** (so not a concurrency limit); pageable H2D
  **10.3-11.8 GB/s**; the engine's own bulk staging reaches **26.1 GB/s**, "i.e. it is at line rate - this is not where
  the missing time is".
- **Decode is host-bytes bound:** at 32K context **483 MB read from host per token, 27.2 ms/token (~17.8 GB/s sustained,
  70 % of line rate)**, with **~29 % of the token time in host-side GEMV**. "Each ~10 GB/token less is worth roughly
  **+4-5 tok/s**."
- **More cache is not better:** cache sweep at a 45 GB VRAM budget - **18 GB starves, 20 GB 38.7, 22 GB 38.1, 25 GB 37.6,
  30 GB 34.5 tok/s**. "Throughput is monotone in host bytes and *not* monotone in VRAM occupancy past the optimum - **a
  fit that fills VRAM can be measurably slower than one that leaves 2-4 GB free**." The refresh interval has its own
  optimum (every 16 positions ~+12 %; 4 and 32 both worse).
- **The specific negative result that bears on our WDDM thread:** "We mlocked 106 GB of weights (verified at line rate in
  isolation) and switched to DMA straight from host memory, bypassing the pageable->pinned bounce: **32.1 vs 32.6 tok/s**
  otherwise identical. The CPU copy and the DMA are already pipelined across staging workers, so the bounce is part of
  the optimal pipeline, not a bottleneck."

That last line is the counterweight the WDDM note needed: **acquiring residency is not automatically worth anything**
when the copy is already pipelined. It does not weaken the per-allocation-ceiling correction (`NEWS-2026-11-01`); it
bounds the prize, on one model, on a host where pinning worked at line rate. The cache sweep is the second independent
reproduction of our reserve result, and its shape - a maximum, then a slope down - is stronger than ours was (we had 3
points).

### 4.1 [ARX][ENGINE] Non-prefix KV reuse loses up to 100 % of context-dependent answers, and the repair budget belongs on the target

**2611.06397 "Where Should a KV-Cache Repair Budget Go? Distractor Structure and Contextual Compatibility in Non-Prefix
Reuse"** (2026-11-04T12:02:58Z). Verbatim: "Non-prefix KV-cache reuse encodes retrieved chunks independently and
concatenates their caches... **Reuse loses 19-100 % of the context-dependent questions that full prefill answers, and it
loses more when the other chunks are of the same kind as the target.** Because the recoverable damage is spread over these
other chunks, restoring a few of them recovers little, and **the budget is better spent on the target**. This works
through contextual compatibility: recomputing the target against the reused cache beats inserting its full-prefill KV" -
measured across three 7B-8B models and four datasets (dialogue memory, open-domain QA, API documentation).

This is the sharpest external statement of why our `reused` / `N checkpoints` declaration exists. Our rows assume that a
prefix-cache hit is quality-neutral; Strata's own `#2518` and `#5192` items and llama.cpp's checkpoint work are all about
the mechanics of reuse, and this paper says the *ceiling* of reuse can be zero on context-dependent questions even when
the mechanism works. **The instrument it implies we lack:** a paired full-prefill-versus-reuse answer comparison on the
same prompt - which our harness can do (same task, cache cleared versus warm) and has never done.

### 4.2 [ARX][ENGINE] Training-free sparse attention for the full-attention layers of hybrid models

**2611.06133 "Accelerating Long-Context Decoding in Large-Scale Hybrid LLMs Without Retraining"** (2026-11-04T08:18:38Z).
Verbatim: "The full-attention layers of large language models make every generated token more expensive than the previous
one... **Hybrid models** reduce this cost by replacing most full-attention layers with linear-attention layers, **but the
first still dominate decoding at long contexts.** We accelerate these remaining layers **without any training or weight
changes** using combined sparse attention: each layer attends to a sliding window of recent tokens, **a pinned prefix, and
a fixed budget of most relevant tokens**... The window size and budget are found on a per-layer basis using a
**multi-objective search**... Across **six hybrid models from three families, from 4B to 397B parameters**."

Our pack family is hybrid with a PLE table, and the paper's "pinned prefix + sliding window + fixed relevance budget"
is the exact set of knobs Strata exposes as `--kv-resident`, `STRATA_KVA` and the prefill attention switches we have
declined or held (`NEWS-2026-11-01a`, `NEWS-2026-11-01`). It is the design justification for a lever we already hold, with
per-layer search as the part we have never done (we tune globally).

### 4.3 [ARX][ENGINE] Expert deactivation is decided by the *weight rule*, not by which experts you remove

**2611.05804 "Matching the Training-Time Weight Rule in Inference-Time Expert Deactivation"** (2026-11-04T01:23:13Z).
Verbatim: "**Expert deactivation at inference time changes both token routing and expert weights**, and its effect on
performance depends on how these changes are implemented. We compare **removal, freezing, remaining-expert renormalization,
and unnormalized re-routing** on identical disabled sets across five instruction-tuned checkpoints from four model
families... With **half of the experts deactivated** according to activation-based rankings, **remaining-expert
renormalization yields the highest perplexity in models trained with raw router probabilities**, whereas unnormalized
re-routing" is better there.

This is the third scan in a row in which a paper says the *weight rule* moves quality while the routing set is held
(`NEWS-2026-11-02` sec. 3.5, 2610.41366: "renormalizing survivors' gates costs 0.8-8.5 points and reverses whether
rerouting beats MoNE"). It is the mechanism behind Strata's own route switches: `STRATA_ROUTE_TAIL_SKIP` (+6.18 % decode,
KL **0.07731**) and `STRATA_ROUTE_PRIOR=1.5` (+22.9 % perplexity at 1.5, +1.1 % at 0.5) both change weights, not only
routes. **Consequence:** an arm that keeps the same experts but changes the weight rule is not quality-neutral, and our
perplexity rows are the right instrument for it - which is why sec. 1.10's GPQA datum and our KL rows must be read as a
pair.
