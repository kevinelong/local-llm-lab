### 2d. The TTFT regression is fixed in a PR that is not merged

before any warmup**, because our 64-token warmup hides it in either direction, and as of tonight the

## 3. Strata: a release that audits its own numbers, and a qwen36 path that is worse than we thought

### 3a. 0.1.46 does not recover the decode loss - three architectures, one named mechanism

Yesterday's §3b concluded that "0.1.46 is not a speed change on a single RTX 50" from **#3189** (a
nine-arm table on an RTX 5090) plus the maintainer's P100/A4000 measurements. **#3252** has since
accumulated three independent reports that say the loss is real and unrecovered, and the issue moved
from **7 to 11 comments** (updated **2026-10-21T06:54:33Z**):

| arch | card | claim | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **sm_86** | RTX 3080 Ti **12 GB** | 0.1.46 is **-11 %** vs **0.1.45.3**: medians **59.83 t/s** (n=9) → **50.20** (round A) / **54.29** (round B) | `litao9417`, **2026-10-20T17:48:06Z** |
| **sm_80** | A100 40 GB | 0.1.46 **does not recover**: **94.5** t/s against **111.3** on 0.1.44.1 (**-15 %**); prefill on par (3,105-3,119 vs 3,056) | `t-sm0`, **2026-10-21T05:47:54Z** |
| **sm_75** | RTX 2080 Ti 11 GB, Linux | 0.1.42 → 0.1.46.2 is **-6.7 %** on chat and **-9.7 %** at 6,345 tokens | `dvvii`, **2026-10-20T22:41:41Z** |

Three details make this a correction rather than an addition:

- **The sm_86 arm is our VRAM class** (12 GB), not a neighbour - and it is a **step regression from
  0.1.45.3 to 0.1.46**, which is precisely the step #3189 measured as flat on sm_120. Two cards, one
  version step, opposite answers; the honest reading is **architecture-dependent**, and our
  architecture is the one with the *only* flat measurement (n=1 worktree matrix) against **three** that
  are not.
- **One mechanism is named, and it is Linux-only.** `dvvii` reports that with the **0.2.x Linux
  file-tier defaults off**, engine 0.1.46.2 **equals 0.1.42 within ±1.3 %** - i.e. the regression is a
  default, not a kernel. **Our box is Windows**, so this mechanism may simply not apply, and a bump
  would test exactly that. `status: evidence-primary` for their measurement, **`lead` for the
  platform transfer**.
- **The maintainer could not reproduce the package-vs-source gap** (comment **2026-10-21T04:31:34Z**,
  repeated in the release's "Known limits"): released Linux CUDA 12 and CUDA 13 engines against a
  `setup.py`-style source build, **5 interleaved pairs**, on an **RTX A4000** and an **RTX 3060**,
  matched **within 1 %** in decode and at 4K/20K. The one difference left is that the packages build
  ggml's CPU part for a **generic AVX2** CPU. So: the packaged engine is not the cause; a default or
  an arch-specific kernel is.

Two instrument facts from the same thread, both free and both useful:

- **`t-sm0` retracted their own earlier number**: yesterday's prefill **-20 %** "did not reproduce
  today; every one of the five arms reads 3,105-3,119, including the no-switch control." A
  prefill regression that existed for one day.
- **`dvvii` discarded a run because it started 49 s after `tools/calibrate.py`** and showed "a
  monotonic **20 → 39 tok/s** climb with wall-clock time **regardless of arm**". That is this lab's
  2026-10-20 q1 lesson (a rate inherits the machine's prior workload) arriving independently, with a
  one-line rule: **a run that starts inside ~a minute of a heavy pass is void, and the climb gives it
  away.** Their method also names a vendor tool, **`tools/ab_engine.py`** with `exe=` and
  **`--rounds 4`** ("each arm runs in each position twice") - the vendor's own answer to the 8-28 %

### 3b. The speed-definition audit, and which of our recorded rows it touches

§1.2 is not a UI tweak; it is the vendor confirming that **four surfaces disagreed** about what
"tok/s" means, with a **632 → 50-75** collapse for agent turns and a **2x** factor for "divided by the
whole request". Our notes quote Strata decode in several places - **70.8-72.4**, **88.4-90.9**,
**61.3-63.0**, **7.2/7.6 → 69.1/83.0**, **47.89 vs 47.78** - and they were read from the **engine's
own per-request line** or from our own timers, i.e. the denominator we chose, not a UI's. So the audit
**validates the discipline** rather than invalidating a number. Two consequences to write down:

- **Never quote a Strata rate from the app footer, the monitor, the model API or the console.** Quote
  the per-request line and say which numerator and denominator were used. This is the same rule as
  "read the loaded configuration back from VRAM, not from the flag" and "a warmed prefill row measures
  prompt-cache reuse".
- **The new third definition is now the vendor's headline** - every token produced divided by decode
  time, including thinking and, with MTP, the *kept* tokens. That is a **different denominator from
  `tokens/wall`**, which is what our harness rows report. Any future Strata number must say which of
  the two it is.

### 3c. The qwen36 path, read end to end - and it is narrower than the gate question implied

**#3508** (created **2026-10-20T20:39:57Z**, updated **2026-10-21T15:42:02Z**, 3 comments) and
**#3303** (created **2026-10-20T01:13:24Z**, 2 comments) together describe the state of the path our
blocked reference-model item would run on. Both are third-party, both have engine logs, and the second
one is a source read.

**It loads.** A user runs **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-UD-Q4_K_XL** (unsloth, **22.85 GB**, **753 tensors**, MTP
block inside the GGUF) through `setup.py --family qwen36 --context 32768` on 0.2.1/engine 0.1.46 and
serves it. Engine log: "`context 32768 cells, prompt chunk 2048, MTP block loaded, 6.98 GiB of VRAM
free`". **So #2894's capability gate is not a binary blocker at 0.1.46** - the empirical answer is
that the family is accepted, which is stronger than the `strata --families` command I proposed
yesterday.

**It is unoptimised in five separate ways, each with a source or a log line:**

- **The expert cache is fixed at 73 of 256 slots** and **all 18.75 GiB of experts sit in pageable host
  memory**, with **6.98 GiB of VRAM left free**. `--expert-vram 13` moved **3** experts (70 → 73).
- **The root cause is a clamp, found in the source** by `visualpha` (**2026-10-21T15:42:02Z**):
  `src/qwen35/q36_engine.cu:2897` computes `slot_bytes` from the budget at L2896 and then, if
  `slot_bytes + staging + 256 MiB > fr`, **overwrites it with `fr - staging - 512 MiB`** - so the
  13 GiB budget never reaches the slot computation, and `--expert-vram 8` equals `--expert-vram 13`.
  On that card `fr = 6.98`, `staging = 1.10`, giving ~5.38 GiB → **73 slots**. **The flag is parsed,
  used once, and silently overwritten** - our exact failure class.
- **The qwen36 path refuses the generic engine's memory knobs**: `strata-q36: ignoring --kv int8 (not
  an option of this engine)`, same for `--kv-grow`, `--mmap-experts`, `--expert-cache`.
- **Its KV is fp32 with no quantised option** (**#3303**): `dalloc()` is `cudaMalloc(n * 4)`
  (`src/qwen35/q36_engine.cu:1127`), and `--kv` / `--kv-resident` / `--spec` "are not parsed at all".
  `--max-context 262144` **OOMs** on a 16 GB card; **32768 and 100000 start**. On our 12 GB card that
  boundary is lower, unmeasured.
- **MTP never fires on Windows.** Every one of 10 recent requests on 0.2.2/0.1.47 logs
  **`drafts accepted 0 of 0`**, while **#3303's Linux log on an RTX 5060 Ti shows `drafts accepted
  237/324, 229/342`** - about **73 %**. `--mtp 0` vs `--mtp 2` changes decode by **29.8 vs 30.2**
  tok/s, "consistent with the draft loop never running", and the MTP block does load (startup 61 s
  with MTP=2 vs 97 s with MTP=0). **And `draft_n` in `timings` is always 0 on this path by
  construction**, because the MTP block is inside the GGUF rather than a separate layer - so the field
  we added to the protocol ("print draft acceptance") **reads 0 whether MTP works or not**. The
  acceptance *log line* is the only witness.
- **0.2.2 changed none of it**: "The `hybrid experts` line is **byte-identical** to 0.2.1".

**Decode measured on that path**, short prompt, 300 output tokens, thinking off: **22-31 tok/s**, median
**31.3**, on a 16 GB Pascal card with `nvidia-smi` at 78-83 % util and no throttle. For comparison the
same reporter cites **126-133 tok/s** on a 16 GB RTX 5060 Ti on Linux with the same model and quant.

**What that means for the queue:** if the gate is open, the honest expectation is **not** a fair fight.
At 32K on our card the path would have fp32 KV, a VRAM-clamped expert cache, no `--kv`, and - on
Windows - **dead MTP**. A Qwen3.6-on-Strata context ladder measured there would be a measurement of an
unoptimised path and **must not be compared to llama.cpp's ~90 tok/s**. It is worth running only as a
"does it answer correctly and at what rate" row, with the five limits declared.

## 7. Research: the index reached 2026-10-20, and three papers land on instruments we run

arXiv's index now reaches **2026-10-20T17:55:44Z** (newest cs.CL submission **2610.28616**), against
2026-10-19T17:59:58Z yesterday. Five in the new day bear on us; three are direct.

- **2610.28577, "Cache Engineering for Agents"** (2026-10-20T17:32:24Z). Agents get two new
  operations on their own KV cache - **`drop`** to exclude selected cache from future attention and
  **`repos`** to adjust retained keys' position encodings - **without modifying the trajectory and
  without re-prefilling the retained context**, which is the cost that makes trimming and summarising
  expensive ("modifying the trajectory prefix invalidates subsequent cached states"). The formal
  finding is the one to keep: **"history dependence as a serving challenge: identical token sequences
  can yield different cache states under different cache operation sequences, making prefix matching
  insufficient for correct cache reuse."** Numbers: a tool-response rolling-drop policy gains **+1.7
  points** on average benchmark score and cuts cost **27.7 %** against threshold-triggered
  summarization; their AKV engine reaches **1.2x** output throughput and **1.5x** P95 latency
  speedup; recursive self-improvement of cache policies cuts a further **7.1 %**. Code:
  `github.com/thu-nics/AKV`. `status: evidence-primary`. **Applies: directly, as the theory behind a
  rule we already follow empirically.** Our 2026-10-20 q2b artifact - "the **first** request after a
  load reports prefill ~99 tok/s and the **second** 50-60, because the second prompt hits the prompt
  cache" - is an instance of prefix reuse, and this paper says prefix matching is **not sufficient**
  to establish that reuse is *correct*. So a cached-prefill row is not only measuring a different
  thing; its **validity** depends on a cache history nobody records. Two consequences: keep taking
  prefill from the **first** probe, and if we ever adopt a cache policy, the identity check is
  "same tokens, same cache operation sequence", not "same prefix".
- **2610.28500, "BitMTP: When Multi-Token Prediction Meets Low-Bit Large Language Models"**
  (2026-10-20T16:42:17Z, **accepted at NeurIPS 2026**). The claim that matters is a **negative** one:
  "although complementary, **the direct combination of these methods cannot accelerate decoding at
  comparable quality: masked drafting lacks intermediate token input supplied in next-token training,
  while wider computation and causal key-value (KV) updates can offset the savings from fewer
  steps**." Their co-design (joint NTP+MTP objective, confidence-only / speculative-only / hybrid
  decoding, "accepted-prefix alignment keeps the committed prefix, **materialized causal KV boundary**,
  and CUDA Graph replay state consistent") reaches **2.2x** on BitNet b1.58-2B and **2.1-2.4x** on
  1.7B-4B **Bonsai** models on an H20 at comparable quality, and up to **2.0x** on an RTX 3090.
  `status: evidence-primary`. **Applies: directly, and it lowers a prior.** Our recorded MTP result
  (**+31.2 % per token, -7.3 % wall, +21.7 % more tokens**, 2026-10-08) is on a **Q4_K_M** backbone;
  every Strata pack we run is **Q2_0 / IQ1_M / Q3_K_M**, i.e. the regime this paper says does *not*
  stack naively. So "MTP did not help" on an ultra-low-bit pack is now the **predicted** outcome, and
  the mechanism ("wider computation and causal KV updates offset fewer steps") is measurable: report
  decode time per accepted token, not just accepted tokens. The paper's third point is a bug class -
  **committed prefix, KV boundary and graph replay must stay consistent** - which is precisely what a
  silently-wrong speculative output looks like from the client. And **Bonsai** is a family we already
  score at 100 % (Swift Bonsai 2 PQ2, 221/221 on the frozen suite), so its 1.7-4B MTP rows are the
  nearest published comparison to a model we own.
- **2610.28491, "OptionWorld: A Benchmark for Diagnosing Failures of LLMs as Long-Term Personal
  Assistants"** (2026-10-20T16:38:43Z). In failed instances **the key facts remain in the model's last
  input before the deadline in 35.5 %** of cases - so the failure is **maintaining task state**, not
  acquiring information. The three most frequent problems: "needed information is not obtained or
  combined", "**available opportunities to act go unused**", and "**agents impose restrictions that
  the task does not require**". Follow-ups: supplying missing facts restores success in some cases;
  **action reminders restart work** but later steps still miss the deadline; and "correcting a
  supplied note that **wrongly forbids contact** restores permitted inquiry". `status:
  evidence-primary`. **Applies: directly to `ledger` and to the state-note rule.** It is the empirical
  companion to **2610.26632** (the 92.5 pp re-observable-state result, covered 2026-10-20): that paper
  separated *can* from *cannot* re-observe; this one says that even when the fact is **already in
  context**, 35.5 % of failures are about **connecting it to unfinished work**. And "agents impose
  restrictions the task does not require" is a fourth failure mode our prose-quit census (50 of 99)
  never named - our families were completion-summaries, truncations, kills and error stops.
- **2610.28498, "Present but Hard to Read: Probing Latent Reasoning with Minimal Pairs"**
  (2026-10-20T16:41:29Z). Probing studies "read a model's state where the answer begins and take a
  failed probe as evidence that a value is absent"; with prompts differing only in a copied digit, the
  digit **is** present at the last prompt token in a shared code (readout about **0.9**) in both a
  looped model (Ouro-1.4B) and a standard transformer (Qwen2.5-1.5B) - so **a single-prompt probe
  under-reports what is there**. `status: evidence-primary`. **Applies: as a caution on instrument
  design**, the same shape as our own rule that a check encoding an assumption measures the
  assumption. Not a queue item.
- **2610.28586, "Capable Teacher, Myopic Scorer: Reward-Free Teacher Repair for Agentic On-Policy
  Distillation"** (2026-10-20T17:40:08Z). Agentic OPD trains a student on its own trajectories while a
  teacher scores **every token**; the failure is described as the student leaving "the region where
  the teacher is reliable". `status: evidence-primary` for the framing. **Applies: as the training-side
  twin of 2610.26797** (covered 2026-10-20: a student inherits the teacher's bias and agreement with
  the teacher cannot reveal it). No queue item.
