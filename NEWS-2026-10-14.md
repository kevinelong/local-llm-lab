## 1. The engine has moved five releases, and two of them are load-bearing for us

We are pinned at **Strata 0.1.40.3**; 0.1.41 was staged, measured neutral, and adopted for its
watchdog/tool-parse fixes only. Since then, published: **0.1.42, 0.1.43, 0.1.43.1, 0.1.44, 0.1.44.1**.

- **0.1.43 claims up to 38% faster local inference** (third-party summary, 2026-10-11:
  `linuxcompatible.org/story/strata-v0143-delivers-up-to-faster-local-inference`). Our measured wall
  is a **43.6:1 read:write, prefill-bound** workload, which is exactly the axis a 38% claim would
  move - and our own 0.1.41 A/B found the upgrade neutral on cold prompt (636 vs 632) and the
  `--expert-cache 1500` variant of it a regression. So this is a **specific, cheap, falsifiable
  test**: 0.1.43.x or 0.1.44.x against 0.1.40.3 on our own ledger/tactics runs, cold prompt included,
  not a general "upgrade and hope".
- **0.1.44's release notes claim answers from the GSQ-RCO packs are byte-identical to 0.1.43.1**
  ("the project's identity gates"), per issue `Niko1221/Strata#2333` (2026-10-13). That is a
  **determinism claim about our exact workload**, and we already have the instrument for it: our
  probes came back byte-identical three times on one prompt, and the placement experiment showed
  prose moves while answers do not. A byte-identity check across 0.1.43.1/0.1.44 is cheap and would
  let us upgrade for speed without re-baselining, or refute the claim.
- Community data points exist but are not comparable to ours: an RTX 3060 12 GB back-to-back
  IQ3_XXS vs IQ3_S comparison (issue #2333), an RTX 5090 + 96 GB rig running 0.1.41 vs 0.1.42 at
  C=1 with a power-limit confound, and an Ivy Bridge port of 0.1.34 serving GSQ-RCO IQ3_S on an HP
  Z820. Same lesson as always: they are leads, not evidence.
- Engine features seen in PRs worth knowing about: **Swift-1.5 IQ3_S support added to setup**
  (PR #1610), and explicit **Maya-M/GSQ-RCO 3.5-bit model choices with immutable Hub revisions and
  verified SHA-256 manifests** (PR #1476), plus an opt-in **GLM CPU path and CPU image encoder**
  (PR #1420). The last one matters to us because our image/vision path has so far been a GPU story.

**New model siblings, same family.** `ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO-GGUF` exists at four sizes with
an `mmproj` for multimodal use, and there is a demonstration video (`sf2xEo8DfOU`, below). A 27B pack
is plausibly loadable on this box in a way the 125B is not, which makes it the first new candidate for
the *served-model* role since the 27B dense comparison the Coder review made. The other new entry is a
community 2-bit fine-tune of that same 27B ("Saluki 27B", video `96WTWX_inRY`). Note the Coder pack's
community traction: an uncensored build of it reports **110,409 downloads in 9 days**, which says the
ecosystem is moving fast around exactly the pack we test with - a reason to pin revisions rather than
trust tags.

## 2. Related projects, and what each is worth to this lab

| project | what it is | what to take |
|---|---|---|
| `zjunlp/OneDayAgent` (MIT, arXiv 2608.05013) | long-horizon harness; 104-task AgentIF-OneDay benchmark; decomposes multi-hour requests into bounded subtasks, verifies deliverables against the original goal, repairs, and compresses execution memory | its three mechanisms are named answers to our own failure list (goal drift, state transfer, context overflow). Its claim - *the harness, not the model, drives long-horizon reliability* - is the same conclusion our census reached from the other direction (**45 of 99 sub-perfect sessions were harness or platform artifacts**). **Docker-based, and we do not add Docker without a decision** - read it for design, not for a runner |
| `Stephanie Jarmak, arXiv 2608.13867` | monograph: *Engineering Reliable Coding Agents: Evaluating and Operating the System Around the Model*; 206 reliability records, 193 gated practices, evaluation protocols | the thesis is our own: "many apparent model failures originate elsewhere in the system". Use it as the external citation for our harness-vs-model attribution discipline, and mine its protocol list before designing the generate-then-run battery |
| `tkarim45/agent-eval-harness` (MIT) | scores tool-using agents on **success, tool-call F1 against a reference trace, step efficiency (optimal/actual), cost per task**; ships a good agent and a deliberately flawed one | the four metrics are exactly the primitives our tables lack (we have tokens, turns, tool counts, but no trace F1, no step efficiency, no cost column). Its **discrimination test** is a methodology move we should copy: competent agent 1.00 vs flawed agent 0.58 on the same scorer - a suite that cannot separate two synthetic agents is rubber-stamping |
| `ahwurm/localharness` (MIT, v0.18.0, ~805 weekly downloads) | model-agnostic agent harness for local LLMs over llama.cpp/Ollama/vLLM/LM Studio; YAML agents, one permission gate, memory and an `autoresearch` plugin that "proposes one harness change, runs it through a statistical gate, adopts or rejects it" | the closest external analogue to our own harness. Two things to read closely: its **fail-closed permission gate** (untrusted content never gets mutating tools) and the **autoresearch statistical gate**, because our promotion gate is the same idea with our own numbers |
| `linny006/agent-eval-harness`, `mkj69/long-horizon-agents`, `RUC-NLPIR/Awesome-Long-Horizon-Agents`, `benchlm.ai` (26 agentic evals ranked) | live benchmarks and research maps over long-horizon agents, terminal-bench/OSWorld/MCP tool use | landscape, not instruments. The maps are the fastest way to see which failure classes other people have already turned into batteries |

## 3. Four transcripts, filed

All four are in `ai_bench/transcript-strata-coder-20261014/` with the fetch scripts; only the derived
ideas are quoted here. (yt-dlp's subtitle endpoint returned 429 for the first one and worked for none
of them; `youtube-transcript-api` fetched all four.)

1. **Coder review** (`09GB4LtXUok`) - the note referenced above; nine tests, three of which name gaps
   we have no battery for.
2. **"Strata $1000 Local AI Build"** (`x7s6Mf4xF9A`) - a quantization ladder on **one pinned generative
   artifact**: an animated SVG of a cat walking a fence, run through Q2_0, IQ2_XS, IQ3_XXS, IQ3_S.
   Q2_0's cat is "barely able to walk" with almost no ears at 15,275 tokens; IQ2_XS broke the scene
   (the fence stopped moving, the background moved) at 85,844 tokens; IQ3_XXS was coherent at 37,815;
   IQ3_S was excellent - parallax, click-to-pause, tempo control, day/night - at 52.4 tok/s. **This is
   the strongest argument yet for the generate-then-run class**: our quantization comparisons are all
   check-based, so no number we publish says "the 2-bit pack produces an artifact that does not hold
   together", even though that is the difference a user sees. It also produces a **token-cost**
   column for free, which varied 5.6x across the ladder.
3. **"Saluki 27B"** (`96WTWX_inRY`) - a 2-bit fine-tune of the 27B advertised at **96% of the full
   model averaged over nine benchmarks**, where the per-benchmark numbers tell a different story
   (AME 2025 96.7 -> 79.2). Its tool-calling win comes from a **120-task test**, and the full model's
   public scores come from **a different harness**. Two of its findings are directly reusable: the
   vision projector shipped but **did not work** (verify the capability, not the card), and a mostly
   sound answer finished with **a confident invented detail** (Australian banks "offer 15 and 25 year
   fixed loans") - a hallucination shape our multiple-choice knowledge instrument cannot see.
4. **"Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO demo / claims audit"** (`sf2xEo8DfOU`) - the most useful of the three new
   ones for methodology, because it audits a quantization table rather than a model:
   - **The headline number was measured without generation.** The "100.3% of the full model" row is
     five multiple-choice quizzes where the model *rates how probable the premade choices look*
     rather than writing an answer. A researcher from the releasing lab is cited saying exactly that.
     This is the same measurement-validity issue we closed for `civil_comments`, arriving from a
     different direction - and it lands on our **knowledge instrument**, which is MMLU-shaped and
     therefore scores choices, not generation.
   - **Quantization cuts code hardest, quizzes hold.** Same table: the smallest file keeps its quiz
     score while losing ~9 points on LiveCodeBench v6 (85.7 -> 76.6), and ~5 on GPQA Diamond
     (89.9 -> 84.9). That is external support for the per-family reporting rule we adopted for the
     bespoke suite, and a reason to keep code batteries and recall batteries in separate columns.
   - **Cross-team numbers are not comparable, and it is measurable.** The lab itself records 85.7 for
     a model that its own authors report at 90.3 on the same named test; SWE-bench Pro numbers come
     from a corrected variant of the test. Our version of this rule is the platform-change rule and
     per-model provisioning - this is the same rule with someone else's numbers.
   - **A reproducible language failure.** On the 10.1 GB file, Chinese prompts produced code that
     would not execute, three rounds of self-repair included, while the same prompt in English
     worked and the 11.8 GB file did better. The lab's reply: ~90% of the calibration text is
     English. We already have a CJK note against the Coder pack (#438, Chinese answers wrong or
     looping); this turns it into a **measurable class** - same task, several languages, artifact
     must run - which fits the generate-then-run idea exactly.

## 4. What this adds to the approved plan, in the order I would do it

1. **Language-conditioned execution.** Same small task in English, Chinese, and one more, each
   scored by running the artifact and asserting a behaviour. Extends the generate-then-run battery
   from one run to a matrix, and prices a failure class we have only as an anecdote.
2. **A quantization-quality ladder on one pinned artifact.** The cat-on-a-fence design, hardened:
   one spec, four packs, scored as (a) it runs, (b) each required behaviour is present, (c) tokens to
   deliver. This is the only proposal here that answers a question users actually ask.
3. **The four trajectory metrics from `tkarim45/agent-eval-harness`** - trace F1 against a reference
   tool sequence, step efficiency, cost per task, alongside success - plus **its discrimination test**
   as an acceptance criterion for the suite itself: before a new battery is adopted, a competent
   synthetic agent and a deliberately flawed one must score differently on it.
4. **Annotate every battery as scoring or generating**, and never publish a mean across the two
   families. We already decided this for the bespoke suite; the knowledge instrument has not been
   annotated, and this scan says it should be.
5. **A capability-presence check for vision**, as a small, cheap item: the mmproj path either works
   or does not.
