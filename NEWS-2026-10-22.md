## 2. VRAM: the constraints, with numbers

### 2.1 A Windows ceiling on pinned host memory: about half of RAM

**VRAM.** `FlashML-org/FreeToken` **#55**, opened **2026-08-22T18:41:17Z**, closed **2026-08-31T06:50:04Z**
- found today by widening the search, not previously in these notes. Environment: Windows 11 native (no
  WSL), **RTX 3080 Ti 12 GB**, driver **591.86** (CUDA 13.1), **32 GB RAM**, FreeToken 0.1.1 and 0.1.2,
  model `ornith-ai/Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B-NVFP4` (~17 GB of host bank sources).

- The offload backend allocates the NVFP4/FP8 source banks **unpinned** and only later maps them with
  `cudaHostRegister`. **WDDM enforces a pageable-locking pool of roughly half of system RAM**, measured
  here at **13.77-13.82 GB**, beyond which the registration fails with `cudaHostRegister failed: out of
  memory` and the whole serve aborts.
- A failed registration does not stay local: every unmapped source left in the copy plan "poisons
  execution later (`CUDA error: invalid argument` surfacing asynchronously at KV-pool init)".
- The designed escape hatch (LOCKED/PAGEABLE residency on the CPU executor, i.e. `VirtualLock` +
  `SetProcessWorkingSetSize` on Windows) is **scaffolded but not implemented** - `HostBank.lock()` and
  `OffloadMoeCache.set_bank_sources` raise `NotImplementedError`.
- Two adjacent findings that matter to us: **the KV pool ignores `--max-seq-len-override`** (a 16K override
  still requested ~146,450 pages, ~2.8 GB bf16), and **`--moe-cpu-layers` does not reduce bank membership**
  at all - the flag cannot be used to get under a memory quota.

**Applies: yes, as a declaration.** This box is 63.46 GiB visible RAM on Windows with a GeForce card, so
the same rule predicts a ceiling near **31.7 GB** `[INFERENCE: half-of-RAM applied to 63.46 GiB]`. Every
resident/`mlock` arm we have measured sits far below it (12 GiB in use, 24 GiB tried once and floor-
tripped), so **nothing recorded is invalidated** - but the ceiling explains why a "pin all the experts"
strategy has an unknown wall in front of it, and it is the missing half of `#30793` (larger than 4 GiB host
buffers were split by `#30449`, so `--load-mode mlock` silently skipped locking them). **Instrument:** the
engine's own log, for a lock failure or its absence. **Cost:** reading a log line - no download.

### 2.2 The KV byte is the context cost, measured twice

**VRAM.** Two independent reports today, both on sm_120-adjacent hardware, both with logs or source:

- **Strata #2464**, newest comment **kirinriki7777-sys, 2026-10-21T18:29:28Z** (thread now 11 comments):
  Ornith-1.5-122B (32 query heads, 2 KV heads, 12 full-attention layers + MTP) on an **L4**, 8 expert
  slots per layer, `--max-context 131072`: decode **6.0 tok/s at 18,671** prompt tokens, **2.8 at 42,204**,
  **1.8 at 83,802**, **1.4 at 116,130**. Time per token grows by **~6.0 ms per 1,000 tokens** of context
  (Qwen3.6 on the same card: **~2.3 ms**), and the ratio **2.6** is close to the ratio of query heads x
  attention layers, **(32 x 13) / (16 x 11) = 2.36**. With **every routed expert in VRAM** (Colab A100
  80 GB, 44.69 GiB): **64.0 tok/s at 2.5K, 35.4 at 18,671, 13.3 at 83,802**, growth **0.72 ms per 1,000
  tokens**, "8.3x less ... close to the ratio of the two cards' memory bandwidth", with fp32 K explicitly
  named as what every query head reads.
- **Strata #3700** (see §4.3) prices the same effect from the other side: the int8 K/V prototype is
  **3.56x** fewer bytes per cell and takes 56K decode from **28.4 to 99.3 tok/s** on an RTX 5080 16 GB.

**Applies: directly, as a model of our own context ladder.** Our q36 numbers (2026-10-20 note: 109.3 /
85.7 / 40.3 / 23.4 / 14.6 tok/s at 256 / 4K / 32K / 65K / 114K) fit the same shape, and the mechanism is
arithmetic rather than mystery: attention decode traffic scales with `n_ctx x query_heads x layers x
bytes_per_value`. **Instrument:** a context ladder reporting decode at 4K/16K/32K/64K **and** the KV
format read back from the startup line. **Cost:** one engine load per rung, ~5-20 minutes - no download if
the engine is already staged.

### 2.4 Two VRAM-floor reports on Blackwell, and what our floor really protects

**VRAM.** Both from the same reporter, both with engine logs and a source read:

- **#3470** (0.1.44.1, 2x RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB sm_120, Linux): free VRAM on the later stage **drops 2-6 MiB
  per long cold prompt and never plateaus** - 204 MiB at load, 142 after the first 115K prompt, then 136,
  132, 128, 126, 122, 118, 116, 112, 110, and **106, 104, 98 on three repeats of the same 113,445-token
  prompt**. The source read (verify graphs keyed by window size, prompt buffers in borrowed cache slots,
  MTP `pf_dev_` a one-chunk high-water mark, KV preallocated for `--max-context`, fixed 32 MiB cuBLAS
  workspaces, no `cudaMallocAsync`) finds **no allocator that grows**, "which leaves state inside the
  driver or cuBLAS, outside the source". Mitigation tried: `--vram-reserve-later-mib 1265`.
- **#3702** (0.2.2, RTX 3060 12 GB + RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB, Windows not stated): `layer_split: auto` leaves
  **68 MiB** on the last card; startup says so (`LOW ... add --vram-reserve-later-mib 1400`), each new
  verify window size captures a few MiB (68 -> 64 -> 58 -> 52 -> 10), then capture fails and the engine
  **exits** - `verify: instantiate: out of memory`, `prefill gemm: cublasGemmEx f16: cuBLAS status 14`,
  **6 engine starts in 11 minutes**, 8 of a `--long` run's requests failed. `--vram-reserve-later-mib 1400`
  gives 770 MiB free and **0 failures in 10 `--long` runs**, at a cost of expert-cache slots on the last
  card (auto K=18 -> 19, CUDA1 2,579 -> 2,343 slots).

**Applies: yes, as a floor justification.** Our **0.5 GiB** free-VRAM floor was chosen from the Strata
ledger's own margin; these two reports say the engine needs **96 MiB of reserved room for window graphs per
later stage** (`kWindowMib`, `src/program/generate.cpp`) and that a card with 68 MiB free **dies**. Our
single-card case has no "later stage", so the floor is not the same variable - but a **2-6 MiB-per-prompt
drift** means a long agent run on one card is also drifting toward its floor, which is a reason to keep
sampling VRAM during a run rather than only at load. **Instrument:** the existing monitor's free-VRAM
series, sampled at 250 ms; the new requirement is to report **first and last** value, not the minimum.

### 2.5 What our card's table actually is

**VRAM.** `docs/MMVQ_IL_TABLE.md` (read today at `main`): rows are per-architecture, and **"sm_86 (Ampere
consumer) and sm_120 cards with fewer than 80 SMs (RTX 5070 and smaller)" share `kIlRowsShared`, measured
on an RTX 3060 and an RTX 5070 together, where "a cell is non-zero only if the worse of the two cards
still gains 3 %"**. Cards with **80+ SMs** (RTX 5080/5090, RTX PRO 5000/6000) get **`kIlRows_120`**
(`STRATA_MMVQ_IL_120=0` reverts), **sm_89 and every other sm_80+ card** get the shared table too and
"nobody has measured sm_89 yet", and **5-8 token windows** have a second table `kIlRows58_<cc>` that
**only sm_120 has**.

**Applies: as a negative result, which is the useful kind.** The sm_80 report that landed today
(`linxuhao`, CMP 170HX: own-row tables worth **decode +2.7 to +3.2 %**, and `STRATA_DEQUANT_WIDE=1` worth
**prompts +6.3 to +7.2 % at 3.3K**, "A100 owners can try that today without a rebuild") **does not
transfer to us**: ours is not a card with no row, it is a card whose row was co-measured with ours. So
"measure a table for the RTX 5070" is **already done by the vendor** and is not a queue item. **Instrument:**
none needed. **Cost:** zero - this is the cheapest item to close today.
