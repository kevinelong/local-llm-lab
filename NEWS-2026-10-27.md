| configuration | prompt tok/s | decode tok/s |
|---|---:|---:|
| expert arena at the engine's WDDM cap (8 GiB, 6 slices) | not measured | 43.3 |

### 2.6 [GH][ENGINE] A prefill lever that promises *identical bits*: skip un-routed streamed experts

**#4282 / #4283** (2026-10-27T05:15Z, both open). Mechanism: a prompt chunk of >= 1,024 tokens streams every
non-resident expert of every layer; the ring holds more entries than a layer has, so a layer's copies are queued
before its router runs and **experts no token routes to are copied anyway**. The proposal, opt-in with
**`STRATA_PREFILL_ROUTED_STREAM=1`** (off by default): queue at most 16 copies ahead, and skip an entry not yet issued
when no token routes to it. Measured on one commit over `main 4b9e05a2` (v0.2.6), RTX 3090 24 GB at PCIe 3.0 x16,
Swift IQ3_XXS, same binary off/on, 4 pairs: **42 % of streamed experts skipped on a 1,046-token read, 22 % on a
1,979-token follow-up, 3 % on a 31K read** - and, the claim that matters, *"every routed expert is still copied, in the
same order, into the same ring slot, so the prompt's results are the same bits."*

- **Applies:** our q2_0 runs stream experts over PCIe with a CPU lane. A 3 % skip at 31K says the lever is
  **short-prompt**, which is where our agent turns live.
- **Instrument:** the prefill half of the 414-check runner - two arms, same prompt, compare prefill tok/s **and** the
  artifact hash (the claim is bit-identity, so the answer-level check is free).
- **Cost:** two engine starts, ~40 min, no download.

### 2.9 What #4261 retro-explains about our own last two commits

Our 2026-10-26 engine work (`1ceeaa6`, `fef955c`, `9c069b2`) ran a **chunk ladder** on the q2_0 pack at 10.3 GiB free
VRAM, read 704/717/720 tok/s prefill (flat), then **withdrew** it when an identical arm repeated at 987.0 against
122.6 and the engine log showed **only 2 GiB of experts resident, 29.6 GiB read unbuffered, "the file cache cannot
keep them"**. #4261 supplies one candidate explanation that costs a grep: if the free-VRAM-sized cache granted too few
slots, the **chunk itself was clamped**, and a ladder of labels measured a constant. The clamp prints a line. We never
looked for it.

**Our numbers against the [YT] rule (sec. 9.1).** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B Q4_K_M carries **16.875 GiB of expert tensors**
(recorded 2026-10-20) and routes **8 of 256** experts per token. A quarter of that pool is **4.22 GiB**; our q2 ladder
read **47.4 / 45.3 / 45.7 / 58.6 / 66.8 / 78.3 tok/s** at **0 / 256 / 512 / 1024 / 2048 / 4096 MiB** and q2c extended
it to **82.6-83.4 at 5,120** and **82.7-83.6 at 5,376 MiB**, with **453 MiB free failing** at 5,632. So our curve tops
out at **5,376 MiB = 31 % of the pool**, just above the video's quarter, and it was **flat-to-negative below 512 MiB**.
The two descriptions agree on where the knee is. They disagree on the video's *85 % hit-rate break-even*, and **we
cannot check that on b11480 because that build prints no cache line at all** - the q2 note from 2026-10-20 recorded
exactly that limitation, and it is why VRAM readback, not hit rate, became our engagement proof.

### 3.3 [GH][ENGINE] The small-cache fix closed, and its threshold is the video's threshold

**#30848** "Handle small moe caches better" - created 2026-10-20, **closed 2026-10-27T12:13:04Z**, author eapache,
AI-usage disclosure YES. Content: warn for caches too small to hold a single token's experts, and replace the hard
SLRU cutover with **a protected set that ramps from 0 % at 1x a token's experts to 50 % at 1.8x** - *"performance

  ladder was measured on **b11480**, which is **LRU**, so the whole curve predates SLRU *and* this fix.
