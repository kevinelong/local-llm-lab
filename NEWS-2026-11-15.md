## 3. [GH] ComfyUI: the cast-buffer leak is a *pin*, not a size - and its fix was proved with the exact metric our image track uses

**`#17122`** (opened 2026-10-17, updated **11-15T05:58:44Z**) and its fix **`#17123`** (5 comments, same minute) are
the day's cleanest instrument finding, and they correct the reading the 11-12 file took from `#17702`.

- The mechanism: **since `#14372`, `reset_cast_buffers()` only runs after a node when `aimdo_enabled` is true**
  (its only remaining call site is inside `if comfy.memory_management.aimdo_enabled:` in `execution.py`). Without
  aimdo, **`LARGEST_CASTED_WEIGHT` holds the largest lowvram-cast weight until the end of the prompt**, and
  because safetensors are mmap'd, **that one small weight keeps the whole file mapping resident**.
- The measurement that makes it a defect rather than a theory: LTX-2.5 on an **RX 9070 XT, ROCm 7.2, 32 GB RAM**,
  Gemma 4 text encoder **15.4 GB int8** loaded partially - **one 15 MB `Linear` stays referenced and pins
  ~13 GB of the encoder's mapping** (`/proc/<pid>/smaps`) while `current_loaded_models` is **empty**. The DiT then
  loads with **~200 MB free** and stalls in `copy_` -> `memcpy_and_sync` with
  `page allocation failure` in `svm_range_restore_work [amdgpu]`, **3 % GPU, 10+ minutes**.
- The fix runs `reset_cast_buffers()` after each node on the non-aimdo path too, and its test line is the one our
  harness is built on: **"Qwen-Image-2.1 t2i and edit: pixel-identical output"**, alongside LTX-2.5 going from
  *stalled* to **100 s** and LTX-2.3 112 -> 90 s, on v0.41.3 / ROCm 7.2 / torch 2.14.
- **Applies with one caveat we must state**: the report's own precondition is `aimdo_enabled=False` (ROCm), and our
  image arms run the aimdo-enabled path, so this is **not** our failure mode by name. What transfers is the
  **symptom pair** - a stall at a few hundred MB free with near-zero GPU utilisation, and a *pin* whose size is
  the file's mapping rather than the tensor's - and the reminder that **three separate ComfyUI memory defects this
  month have each had a different mechanism and each passed our floor**.

Two papers from the frozen batch that no earlier file here has cited, both on the axis our decisions run on:

- **`2611.12634`**, *"How Far Does Second-Order Linear Response Predict Quantization Mismatch? Pre-Registered
  Measurements on Five Language Models"*. A low-cost screen predicts a quantised model's KL from
  `0.5 d^T F d` (the Fisher expansion under Hessian-aware quantisers), tested with channel-wise int8, per-tensor
  fp8 and group-wise int4 on Qwen3-8B/4B/32B, OLMo-2-7B and **Qwen3-30B-A3B**. Four results that change how a
  quantisation row should be written: **relative predictions hold across a 16x range of perturbation scales and
  int4 group sizes, absolute predictions are heavy-tailed**; **the MoE model exhibits nonquadratic response at
  small perturbations, with int8 KL scaling as `alpha^0.63`** - i.e. the standard second-order screen is wrong
  precisely on the architecture we run; **computing per-sequence gradients in bf16 can cause fourfold prediction
  errors**, traced to the lower half of the backward pass and fixed by fp32 there; and - the number this lab has
  been missing - **"batch-invariant kernels run at 0.55 times the throughput, while changes in reduction order
  introduce a floor of 0.17-0.22 nats per sequence"**. That is a **price and a floor for determinism in one
  sentence**: the price is ~1.8x slower, and the floor is non-zero even with identical flags. Our 11-13 note
  adopted `2611.15671`'s **0-0.92 % of decisions** as a non-quantisation noise floor; this is the same fact in
  nats per sequence, and the two now bracket each other.
- **`2611.11079`**, *"When Calibration Data Matter in Sub-4-Bit Language Model Quantization"*. Task-informed
  calibration (FragCal-DC) over a 2-4 bit sweep, six dense models 1.7B-8B, ten tasks, four-seed comparisons:
  **at two bits, SignRound V2 gains 1.83 pp mean ten-task accuracy over generic-text calibration**, iterative
  methods improve at 2 bits while **single-pass GPTQ and AWQ show small mean responses at 3 bits**, and at 70B
  both improve at 2 bits. The two controls are the honest part: **recovery concentrates on families included in
  calibration**, and **incorrect answer designations retain about 90 % of the mean accuracy gain** - so the effect
  is real but partly circular - and **changes in reconstruction error do not consistently track accuracy gains**.
  **Applies to our own null result**: the 1,100-item comparison that read **88.5 % (Q4_K_M) against 89.7 %
  (Q2_0)** used packs whose calibration text is not declared by either publisher. This paper says at 2 bits that
  text is worth ~1.8 pp, which is larger than the 1.2-point gap we measured and comparable to the **1.33 pt
  paired SE** - so **the calibration set is a declared pack field**, not a footnote.
