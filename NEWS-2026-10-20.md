## 3. Strata: a stable release, a tracker reset, and four measurements that change our shortlist

No PR list is meaningful under a reset, so this section is organised by what a lab decision needs.

### 3a. The reset, and how to read a closed Strata issue from now on

The maintainer's message (**2026-10-19T15:50-15:57Z**, quoted in full in §1.3) is a maintenance
triage of **>1,300** open issues and PRs, not a set of fixes. The reopen path is mechanical: retest
on 0.2.0-beta, retitle with an area prefix, fill the form, comment `/reopen`, and a bot checks it -
**#3075** passed that check at **2026-10-20T14:58:03Z** and is open again. **Consequence for our
notes:** the 2026-10-18 and 2026-10-19 scans quote those issues as live state; they are now closed,
and their content is still true unless a source says otherwise. §3d is that source check for the two
text-level defaults; the rest stay `status: lead` exactly as recorded.

### 3b. 0.1.46 for a single RTX 50: no speed, one fit-check change

**#3189** (§1.4) is the number to quote: **+9.5 % at 16K and +10.7 % at 32K for 0.1.45 → 0.1.45.3**,
and **nothing from 0.1.45.3 → 0.1.46** on a single 5090. The author notes that this "matches the 0.2.1
release notes, where the new defaults are for AMD, Linux, Intel, integrated GPUs and two-card
setups".

The counterweight is **#3252** (created **2026-10-19T20:14:37Z**, 7 comments, open): on an **A100
(sm_80)** 0.1.45 costs **-9 %/-10 % decode and -20 % prefill** against 0.1.44.1 at identical
arguments, and 0.1.45.3 inherits it; the suspects are the **MMVQ/IL shared row table**
(`native_mmvq_il_rows_for` has a `kIlRows_70` entry and everything else inherits `kIlRowsShared`,
**measured on an RTX 3060 and an RTX 5070**), i.e. our card is inside the guess. The release notes
disagree for the cards they own: on a P100 and two A4000 the maintainer measured 0.1.45 against
0.1.44.1 at **x1.015 / x0.992 decode** and **x1.004 / x1.022 prompt**, and the **0.2.1 tip 7-10 %
faster in decode than 0.1.45** on both, finding no sm_80-specific commit. **So: 0.1.46 is not
disqualified for our card by decode rate on the only measurement in our class, and the A100
regression is real on a card we do not have.** `status: evidence-primary` for both tables,
**one machine each**.

New defaults that touch our geometry, from the notes themselves:

- **The draft layer now counts in the fit check and the expert-cache sizing** (about **1 GB** of VRAM
  with MTP on). **Applies: yes** - our Strata runs recorded min GPU free **0.31-0.95 GiB** against a
  **0.5 GiB** floor with one trip, so a version that reserves 1 GB more for MTP moves a marginal
  configuration to a tripping one.
- **`--vram-reserve-mib` you set is kept**, "what you set wins over what the app generates"
  (#3215/#3270/#3216). **Applies: yes, in the good direction** - our configs declare the reserve
  explicitly, and 0.2.1 stops the app rewriting it.
- **Worker counts**: setup now counts **physical** cores with a cap of 32 (`--pool-workers`), while
  the app's **"Work" profile still uses `os.cpu_count()` = logical threads** - **#3417**
  (created **2026-10-20T11:09:51Z**) shows an 8-core/16-thread box getting `--pool-workers 12` from a
  profile whose stated purpose is to *limit* workers. **This box is 24 cores / 24 logical (checked
  today, `Win32_Processor`: `NumberOfCores` 24, `NumberOfLogicalProcessors` 24), so the two rules
  give 24 and 19** - either way, **a Strata A/B must pin `--pool-workers` explicitly**, and the value
  differs between setup, the app profile and the engine default.
- **Still stated as open in the notes:** Windows installers are **not code-signed**; nothing has run
  on a real AMD card on Windows; **`strata bench run` implements their own benchmark protocol, which
  is still a draft**, and the first real run on a release engine is still to come.

### 3c. The reference model on the q36 engine: a context ladder that ends in single digits

**#2464**'s table (§1.5) is the first dense measurement of **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on Strata's own qwen36
path**, and it is not the flat ~90 tok/s the llama.cpp column shows. Three details make it
actionable rather than a complaint:

- **The mechanism is named and is in the setup source.** `setup.py` at v0.2.1 declares the q36 KV as
  **fp32**: `kv_bytes_per_token = 11 * 2 * 256 * 2 * 4` = **45,056 B/token** (11 layers + MTP block,
  2 KV heads × 256, K and V, 4 bytes). At our card's recommended context - `own_path_context()` gives
  **32,768 for ≤16.5 GB of VRAM** - that is **~1.4 GiB** of KV before any weights; at 65,536 it is
  **~2.8 GiB**, on a 12 GB card. The issue says exactly this: "the q36 engine still stores the K/V
  cache in FP32", and the int8-KV PR that helped (**#2698**, 52.5 vs 17.3 tok/s at 65,536) "was
  closed in the reset, not merged".
- **llama.cpp holds to 16K on the same file** (92.2 / 92.1 / 91.3 / 87.3 tok/s at 256/1K/4K/16K), so
  this is an engine property, not a model property.
- **#2894's gate is decided by the engine binary, not by setup's table.** `setup.py` calls
  `strata --families` and fails if the family is absent (`engine_families`, line 6779; the gate at
  6995). At v0.2.1 setup ships a full **qwen36 own-path family** (Unsloth GGUFs, MTP drafts `mtp: 2`,
  vision projector, `--prefill auto`, recommended context from the VRAM rule), and `MIN_ENGINE`
  (0,1,45) carries the comment "v0.1.44: … **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B**". **Whether the 0.1.46 Windows
  prebuilt answers `qwen35moe` to `strata --families` is one command and is unverified here** - it
  is a run, and this scan runs nothing. `status: lead` for the gate, `evidence-primary` for the setup
  source.

**What that means for the queue:** the blocked item ("the reference model on the second engine") is
no longer blocked only by a capability gate. If it clears, the honest expectation is a **decode
ladder that falls with context** (40 tok/s at 32K, 23 at 65K on a V100), on a 12 GB card whose
recommended context is 32,768 with fp32 KV. That is still a fair comparison at 4K-16K, and a
misleading one at 65K.

### 3d. The two text-level defaults: one mechanism still in the source, one unverified

I re-read the source at tag `v0.2.1` rather than trusting the reset:

- **`presence_penalty` is still inherited at temperature 0.** `serve/server.py:4675` builds the
  request's sampling block as `{**model_card_sampling(self.model_arch, thinking),
  **self.sampling_defaults, **self.shared}`, and `MODEL_CARD_SAMPLING` (lines 7594-7597) gives
  **`presence_penalty: 1.5`** to `qwen4exp`'s instruct set and to **both** `qwen35moe` sets. So a
  request that sends no sampling still inherits 1.5 - including `temperature: 0` - and **#2940's
  mechanism is present in 0.1.46 even though the issue is closed**. What the reporter relied on stays
  true: pinning `presence_penalty` in the config's `sampling` block wins, because it is merged after
  the card set. `status: evidence-primary` (source read today).
- **A second, in-request sampling change is visible in the same file** (lines ~4985-4995): when
  repeated reasoning is detected, the server retries with **`temperature` raised to at least 1.0 and
  `presence_penalty` to at least 1.5**, "only the two settings that keep the same words coming".
  **Applies: yes as a declaration** - a Strata run can have its sampling changed mid-request without
  the client asking, which is the same class of hazard as the inherited card set, and it is the
  engine-side twin of our own loop observations (the ledger's 3,043× repeated sentence, Strata's
  loop-break note). I make **no claim about when it arrived**; only that it is in the 0.1.46 source.
- **The prefill chunk is unverified for 0.1.46.** **#2856**'s root cause was 0.1.45's new default
  chunk (4096/2048/1024), with `--prefill 512` giving 4/4 correct and ~17 % slower decode on that
  card. The **0.2.1 notes do not mention a prefill chunk at all**, and `setup.py` still writes
  **`--prefill auto`** for the qwen36 path (line 7028). So the neutraliser remains declarable and
  untested here: **`--prefill 512` in a Strata run config**.

### 3e. Two more details the release exposes

- **The shipped Coder expert profile is stale, and the mechanism bounds what a profile is worth.**
  **#3448** (§1.6) reads `rank_learned_profile` (`src/core/expert_cache.cpp:160-164`): the cache
  sorts by (1) already resident, (2) the learned **heat**, and only then (3) the shipped profile as a
  tie-breaker - so "the heat term re-learns the hot experts within the first minutes". Measured: a
  profile rebuilt from the new model's traces gives **43.6** tok/s against the stale profile's
  **44.8**, "inside the ±5 % run-to-run spread of this box". **Applies: directly to item 4** - it
  predicts a *small* effect for our cold-vs-cold expert-profile pair, and it gives the reason (the
  profile is the third sort key, not the first).
- **A generated text can stop the engine.** **#2236** (open, updated **2026-10-20T11:47:18Z**): if
  the model's output contains the **literal `<|im_end|>` string**, generation stops with
  `finish_reason: "stop"` and **empty or truncated content**, so from the client it looks like "the
  model thought, then gave no answer". **Applies as a scoring hazard** - it is a text-content trigger
  that produces a clean-looking stop, the same shape as our truncation class, and it is model
  independent in the report.

## 7. Research: the index reached 2026-10-19, and the first paper is about our own harness split

arXiv's index now reaches **2026-10-19** (newest cs.CL submission **2610.26860**, 17:59:58Z),
against 2026-10-16 yesterday. Five papers in the new day bear on instruments we run:

- **2610.26632, "When the harness decides success: state that cannot be re-observed"**
  (2026-10-19T15:59:13Z). A pre-registered synthetic probe: truncating the context window costs a
  low-cost reasoning model **nothing in success when a needed fact can be looked up again** (it looks
  it up instead), but **drops success to chance when the fact cannot be re-observed** - the two
  penalties differ by **92.5 percentage points (90 % CI [89.5, 95.5])**; replicated on one model from
  a second provider, and "once the fact was lost, that model chose no answer rather than guessing."
  `status: evidence-primary`. **Applies directly**, three ways. (i) It is the cleanest statement of
  why our `longctx` battery (a fact inside a 222 KB module that can be re-read) and our `ledger`
  battery (a rule that must be carried) fail differently and should not be scored as one axis.
  (ii) "Chose no answer rather than guessing" is a third outcome our decision screen's binary cannot
  express - the same gap the 10-16 prompt-injection paper opened. (iii) It is the argument for the
  state-note-per-turn rule this lab already adopted: the fact that cannot be re-observed is the one
  the harness silently deletes.
- **2610.26797, "Teacher Supervision, Fixed Representations and Calibration: Exact Risk Calculations
  for Candidate-Scoring Decision Models"** (17:42:24Z). Analyses **Jev** and its open counterpart
  **JevAny**, i.e. the exact model family our decision track screens, in Gaussian surrogates where
  finite-sample risk is computable: a student inherits the **teacher's bias**, which "more student
  data does not remove and agreement with the teacher does not reveal"; a frozen representation helps
  only below an explicit sample-size threshold; and in a probit model the recalibration map is a
  temperature. `status: evidence-primary`. **Applies to the `score` primitive**, which is our weakest
  measured axis (`score` 28.7-52.6 macro across five models) and the one where `lev` under-predicted
  the top of a 5-point scale (2.54 against 3.61). The paper's point is that agreement with the
  teacher cannot certify the student - which is what a 0.85 threshold sweep implicitly assumes.
- **2610.26827, "KEEP-Bench: Evaluating Evidence Preservation in Context Compression"** (17:55:18Z):
  4,102 cases over three settings (plausible reference resolutions, explicit qualifiers, minority
  perspectives), and the finding that QA accuracy **underestimates** faithfulness loss because "a
  reader can still answer correctly from an unfaithful compression"; even with tailored prompting the
  strongest models stay unreliable at an average input of only 3.1K tokens. **Applies to `longctx`**
  and to any summarising step: score the evidence kept, not only the answer produced.
- **2610.26722, "Copying Under Repetition: From Prefix Matching to Position Hashing"** (16:50:03Z):
  a constructive result - frontier models "overrun or truncate long blocks of repeated characters",
  and a two-layer transformer with prime-period RoPE-style rotations can copy and **terminate
  correctly under arbitrary repetition**. `status: evidence-primary`. **Applies as the mechanism
  behind two things we already measure**: our ledger's repeated-sentence loop (3,043 repetitions) and
  Strata's own repeat-recovery path (§3d), which is an engine-side patch over exactly this failure.
- **2610.26650, "From Outcomes to Components: A Counterfactual Evaluation Framework for LLM-Proposed
  Repairs"** (16:08:46Z): scores repair actions by **which records** they change and **by how much**
  separately, executing each pairing from the same initial state; in traffic networks, **76 of 114**
  actions gain more from correcting both parts than the sum of the parts, and 6 that get worse under
  either correction alone improve when both are fixed. **Applies to `repo`/`repo2`**: our batteries
  are all-or-nothing per check, and this is a worked design for scoring a repair's target set and its
  update separately, which is the axis `repo2`'s 55 checks approximate with 4 labelled classes.

Two 10-16 papers already covered (2610.23818's acceptance-vs-length inflation, 2610.23787's
agentic draft-acceptance loss) have not changed state and earn no re-coverage here.
