### 1.2 the mechanism, and the two log lines that make it a declaration

Verbatim from `#31233`: "instead of two classes based on whether an expert is new or has had a cache hit, we introduce
**eight classes** ([MQ algorithm inspired]) with a **decaying CRF score**", where "a CRF score (Combined Recency and
Frequency from the LRFU paper) is the number of uses that fades over time":

```
new CRF = old CRF x 2^(-elapsed_tokens / 128) + 1
```

The class of the maximum is derived, not chosen: `CRF_max = 1/(1 - 2^(-1/128)) = 185.165`, `floor(log2(...)) = 7`, so
**8 classes cover the whole range** at a half-life of 128 tokens (`2^-1/128 = 0.9945994`). "For the timescale, we use the
number of decode tokens processed by the expert's layer" and "**Prefill skips this entirely**". Eviction picks "the oldest
eligible experts across all classes and evicts whichever has the largest class-normalized eviction score". Its own stated
gotcha: "uses are computed **per ubatch** (under 32 tokens), so even if a batch selects the same expert for ten tokens,
its score goes up by only one".

The hit-rate and per-layer lines that come with it, from a tester's paste in the same thread:

```
CUDA0 MoE cache size =  1391.58 MiB for 23981.25 MiB of host experts
14 layers, iq4_nl:   208 slots (5.8%, 1.5 tokens worth)
 5 layers, q2_0:      73 slots (5.7%, 1.5 tokens worth)
llama_moe_cache: ubatch <= 8: hits = 415354, misses = 562095, hit rate = 42.49%, uploaded = 1097000.37 MiB
llama_moe_cache: ubatch  > 8: hits = 17,     misses = 1957,   hit rate = 0.86%,  uploaded = 3884.01 MiB
```

**Why this is the item of the day for us and not a curiosity.** Three facts line up:

1. Our cache ladder (`NEWS-2026-10-20`) was measured on **b11480 = LRU**, and `NEWS-2026-10-30` recorded the SLRU
   collapse at one token of slots. **ClassLRU is the third policy**, and its author states exactly where it is worse than
   the second: "what it trails the ramp-down SLRU policy is at the smallest caches: **1.0-1.1x of single-token
   capacity**". `NEWS-2026-10-31` converted our rungs into that unit - **0.47 / 0.95 / 1.90 / 3.79 / 7.59 / 9.96 tokens'
   worth** - so our **512 MiB rung (0.95) is the cell inside the known-bad band** and our 1024 MiB rung (1.90) is
   outside it. That is a prediction our existing data can test without a new arm.
2. **`ubatch > 8` reading 0.86 % is a mechanical statement about what a cache can do for prefill.** Our ladder's prefill
   gains at small caches (72 -> 93 -> 101 tok/s by 512 MiB) came from somewhere else, so either that path differs on
   b11480 or the line is newer than the behaviour; the way to settle it is to read both lines on one build.
3. The **`tokens worth`** field retires the conversion arithmetic this lab did by hand. A rung can now be declared as
   "1.9 tokens' worth" instead of "1024 MiB", which is the unit that transfers between models.

## 2. [GH][VRAM] `#30966` still open - and it now prices the CPU share on three machines, one of them ours

`#30966` ("llama_moe_cache: run missed experts on CPU, add tuning script") is **open**, updated
**2026-11-06T10:51:58Z**, **55 comments**. `NEWS-2026-10-27` recorded it as blocked with "the maintainer's own line ...
*I'll take a look soon - just want to wrap the KV rows first*"; the KV rows work landed as `v0.9.0`'s
`flash_attn_ext_rows`, and the PR has since **grown a calibration script** rather than shrinking.

The script is the finding. `python3 scripts/moe-cache-tune.py -- build/bin/llama-server ...` runs six configurations
(2 cache sizes x 3 shares), reads the model once, and prints the **machine's own constants** before predicting:

```
Machine
    uploads  CPU experts       CPU part       overlap  rest of the token
  9.6 GiB/s   48.7 GiB/s  2.99 ms/token  -19.9 ms/GiB      6.82 ms/token
```

Three machines, all `-cmoe`, all on our model family:

| machine | card / RAM | model | no-cache baseline | small cache, share 0 | best cell |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| author (desktop) | - | Qwen3.8 IQ4_NL, 64,800 MiB experts | - | **6464 MiB, 0: 24.2** | 19392 MiB, 0: **65.5**; 19392 MiB, 0.95: **54.1** |
| @kudraw | RTX PRO 4500 Blackwell 32 GB | GLM 5.3 Flash IQ3_XS | 39 moe, 0 MiB: **14.6** | 40 moe, 4500 MiB, 0: **13.35** | 41 moe, 7000 MiB: **17.65** |
| @satoyon | **RTX 3090 24 GB**, 96 GB RAM, c 262144 | Qwen3.8 UD-IQ4_XS | - | 5632 MiB, 0: **19.5** | 8192 MiB, 0.95: **32.4**; with `--load-mode dio` 5632, 0: **32.1** |
| @MichaelDietzel | **2080 Ti 11 GiB**, `-cmoe -c 32768 -t 22 --load-mode none` | **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B UD-Q6_K_XL** | **42.11** | 2752 MiB, 0: **23.5** | 6784 MiB, 0.95: **64.5** |

Four things this settles or re-opens for us:

- **A cache smaller than ~1 token's worth is not a smaller gain, it is a loss**, and here it is a *large* one: 42.11 ->
  **23.5 tok/s** is **-44 %** against having no cache at all. That is the same sign as our own dead zone at 256/512 MiB
  (`NEWS-2026-10-20`: 45.3/45.7 against 47.4 with no cache) but an order of magnitude bigger, on a card one step below
  ours. **Our ladder's rung-0 arm is therefore not a fair "control" for a small-cache arm**; the controls differ by
  configuration, not by capacity alone.
- **The share's sign depends on the cache size, not on the machine.** At 6464 MiB the desktop arm goes 24.2 -> 47.6
  (+97 %) with share; at 19392 MiB it goes 65.5 -> **54.1 (-17 %)**. @satoyon's `dio` arms do the same within one cache
  size: 32.1 -> 35.9 -> **33.4**. So "raise the share" is not a lever, it is a term in a product, and any A/B must hold
  `--moe-cache-mib` **and** `--load-mode` fixed.
- **`--load-mode dio` changes the memory budget.** @satoyon: 5632 MiB works, **8192 MiB crashes** with
  `cudaMalloc failed: out of memory` during vision warmup, then a cryptic
  `CUDA error: the requested functionality is not supported` in `cublasGemmEx`; `common_fit_params: failed to fit
  params ... tensor_buft_overrides already set by user, abort` explains why nothing shrinks. The maintainer's reply is
  "that's an OOM, probably need to adjust params to use a little less VRAM". **A dio arm needs its own cache rung.**
- **The script's own VRAM estimate is wrong with `--mmproj`.** Its `device_room()` sums only `model|KV|RS|compute|output`
  buffers, so the vision encoder's footprint is unaccounted and "the script pick[s] cache sizes that don't fit". The
  reporter's fix is to parse `[mtmd] estimated worst-case memory usage of mmproj is ... MiB`.

**Instrument that would test this here:** our own cache-rung runner (`chunk_ladder.py`'s slot/VRAM readback discipline)
plus **VRAM readback as the engagement proof** (`NEWS-2026-10-20`: b11480 printed no cache line, so VRAM was the only
evidence). **Cost:** one gated download of a build past `v0.9.0` (>= b12171 per the merges above), then 6 arms at a fixed
prompt with `--moe-cache-mib` x `--moe-cache-cpu-share`. Calling `scripts/moe-cache-tune.py` itself is **6 server starts**
and replaces our hand ladder with the project's own six cells - **~40 min**, no model download (it reads the model we
already stage). That is the cheapest high-information run on the board.

## 3. [GH][VRAM] Strata `#5391`: the tier reversal, measured, plus the 0.1.57 disk-path regression

`#5391` ("experts: a managed cold tier below the RAM budget (scope proposal)", 2026-11-06T08:58:47Z, **0 comments**) is a
proposal, not a change - but it is the best *measurement* in this window, and two of its numbers land on our own rows.

Its A/B (2x RTX 4060 Ti 16 GB, i5-10600KF, 96 GB RAM, Windows 11; RAM-constrained arms "simulated by allocating and
touching memory down to the target free RAM"):

| model / regime | pure mmap | ranked warm tier | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| UD-Q4_K_XL, 59 GiB complement, budget 46 GiB | 11.5 | **20.2** | **+76 %** |
| UD-Q4_K_XL, budget 35 GiB | 11.5 | 14.0 | +22 % |
| UD-Q4_K_XL, full warm (60 GiB) | 11.5 | **27.7** | **+142 %** |
| IQ3_S, 16 GB free RAM (28.5 GiB complement), budget 2 GiB | 18.8 | 23.4 | +24 % |
| IQ3_S, 16 GB free, **1M context**, 130K-token recall | 11.0 | 13.9 | +26 % |
| IQ3_S, 32 GB free, 1M context, recall | 9.1 | 11.9 | +31 % |

**The reversal, and why it is the part to keep:** at **512K** on the same machine "the page cache covers the hot subset of
a 28.5 GiB complement, and **the tier loses** (measured: 16 GB RAM + IQ3_S, 512K: **mmap 24.5 vs tier 23.1 tok/s,
-6 %**; the same scenario at 1M: tier +26 %)". And the opposite failure: "UD-Q4_K_XL at 1M with a **13 GiB budget** that
covers only ~22 % of the complement went **-9 %** versus mmap - a pinned tier too small to matter steals RAM from the
page cache serving the rest". The re-read factor is stated as bytes: "the pure-mmap UD-Q4_K_XL arm read **797 GB** from
the SSD while the 46 GiB warm-tier arm read **64 GB** ... the OS file cache re-reads the same cold experts over and over
(**12x** the bytes for the same work)".

**This is our own resident ladder's shape with the sign made explicit.** `NEWS-2026-11-01` measured resident 2 -> 16 GiB
at **+11.4 then +19.6 tok/s per GiB** on prefill, with a 100 %-uncovered lend region; `NEWS-2026-10-31`'s reserve sweep
showed a GiB of cache is worth ~5x a GiB of resident for decode. `#5391` adds the missing term: **a resident budget that
is too small, or sized for a context you are not running, is worse than mmap** - "four things the engine can see and the
person cannot".

**The 0.1.57 datum.** Under "Relation to the 0.2.11 CPU-share change", measured: "0.2.11's CPU/upload share ... speeds up
the *GPU-side* miss path ... but it does **not** help the *disk-side* miss path these arms live in: both cold-tier arms'
recall decode dropped ~20% on 0.1.57 (16 GB arm: mmap **11.0 -> 8.7**, tier **13.9 -> 10.1**), and the tier's advantage
held (+26 % -> +16 %, +31 % -> +26 %). The two mechanisms sit on different bottlenecks (PCIe/CPU vs SSD bandwidth)".

`[INFERENCE]` This is the **third independent 0.1.56/0.1.57 slowness observation** - after `#5115` (RTX 5080, -25.1 %,
`NEWS-2026-11-04`) and `#5199` (chunk default, `NEWS-2026-11-04`) - and the first on the **low-RAM/streamed** path rather
than the fully-cached-GPU path. It also **bounds yesterday's top-ranked lever**: the +102 % share row in 0.2.11's notes is
a **GPU-side** mechanism, so for a pack whose experts are read from disk it may not appear at all. Our bump declaration
must therefore carry **two** A/Bs, not one, and the share A/B must be run on a configuration whose expert cache actually
holds the working set.

### 4.2 `ggml-org/llama.cpp` `#31777` - Vulkan TDR 0x116 on Windows, avoided by `-ub <= 64`

(2026-11-06T03:38:41Z, 0 comments, unlabelled beyond `bug-unconfirmed`.) Windows 11 25H2 build **26200.9711**, i5-13600K,

and affected physical GPU have not been established", the exact failing value above 64, and no controlled trials.

**Fourth Windows GPU-timeout witness in a month** (`NEWS-2026-11-01`: `sfnx #13`'s WDDM watchdog on 2x 4090;
`NEWS-2026-11-02`: **Strata #5311**'s hard-lock on a gpu-unlock'ed 3090; `NEWS-2026-11-04`: ComfyUI `#17416`). Not our
