Scope: what moved since `NEWS-2026-10-17.md`, in the engines we run and their neighbours, the model
packs, the literature, the related projects and the release feeds. **Reading only.** Nothing was
downloaded, installed, built or run; no engine was started; no gate was checked; no inference and no

Baseline discipline, as the standing rule requires: a release, pack or paper already covered earns a

## 1. What changed, and what each one forces

1. **The Strata 0.1.45 decode regression is no longer one user's report.** **#2936** (created
   2026-10-17T17:13Z, updated 2026-10-18T10:23Z, 3 comments) bisects it to **0.1.43 → 0.1.44.1 →
   0.1.45** on a single RTX 3090 with the same Qwen3.8-Flash-Next family we serve: **−6 %/−8 % at
   0.1.44.1, and −23 %/−27 % at 0.1.45**, with toolchain, llama.cpp submodule, cmake pins and the
   CPU expert pool all held equal. Yesterday's file carried #2453 (Windows, −16-20 % between 0.1.42
   and 0.1.44.1) as the strongest argument against bumping Strata for speed; #2936 is a second,
   independently obtained instance on a different OS and card, and it names the suspect. **Decision it
   forces: "do not move Strata to 0.1.45 for a measurement run" is now evidence-backed, not cautious.**
   `status: evidence-primary` (a controlled A/B/A' with an interleaved retest).
2. **A 0.1.45 sampling default changes answers, at temperature 0.** **#2940** (created
   2026-10-17T17:51Z, updated 2026-10-18T12:33Z, 4 comments) - non-thinking requests inherit the model
   card's **`presence_penalty: 1.5`**, including requests that send `temperature: 0`, and it costs about
   a quarter of decode. Reproduced on **Windows + RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (sm_120)** with the pinned
   `server.py:4193` merge order quoted. **This is the first Strata regression in this scan family that
   changes generated text rather than only rate**, and it is the class our own A/B discipline already
   treats as decisive. `status: evidence-primary` (code path cited) + `lead` (it is one bug, not a
   general statement about 0.1.45).
3. **A standard quantizer now publishes Kolibri-1 GGUFs.** `bartowski/Kolibri-1-GGUF`, last modified
   **2026-10-18T03:32Z**, 87 files, `apache-2.0`: **Q3_K_M 37,620,641,664 B (35.04 GiB)** and
   **Q4_K_M 49,759,219,584 B (46.34 GiB)**, each with a published `.layout.json` and
   `.tensor-types.txt`. Our staged file is Hob-forge's Q3_K_M at **37,523,996,640 B** - the same quant
   name, ~96.6 MB (92.2 MiB) apart, produced before upstream support existed. Yesterday's highest-value item was
   "does our Hob-forge file load in stock llama.cpp ≥ b11712?"; there is now a second, standard-quantizer
   file to fall back on, and a size delta that is a hint (not a measurement) about the tensor set.
   `status: evidence-primary` (HF API, sizes from `siblings[].size`).
4. **The Colibri "price our own GGUF" item was partly answered by someone else, and the answer is
   specific rather than general.** Colibri **#2189** (opened 2026-10-18T08:14Z, 2 comments) reports
   Windows + a `qwen4exp` GGUF at **1.5-1.9 tok/s decode against llama.cpp**, plus two import bugs.
   The maintainer reads the tensor headers and answers that it is **quantisation-specific**: IQ4_NL and
   Q6_K run through the **16-row interleaved** kernels at **80-87 GB/s** on 16 Zen 4 cores, while
   **IQ2_S runs through a per-row kernel at ~16 GB/s**; **#2198** lifts IQ2_S 2.59 → 3.19 and IQ3_S
   2.21 → 4.26 GB/s per core with bit-identical results. Our Qwen3.6-35B-A3B pack is **Q4_K_M**, which
   the reply does not name. `status: evidence-primary` (the maintainer's own reading of the file).
5. **An answer about the third-party harness that matters to us arrived as a release pair:**
   `ahwurm/localharness` went **v0.18.1 → v0.19.1** (v0.19.1 published **2026-10-17T15:06:13Z**), and
   0.19.0/0.19.1 are about **pricing a launch from the model's own tensor table** - dense weights, KV
   and projector on the GPU, an MoE's experts in RAM when RAM holds them, `-c` and `--cache-ram` derived
   from that, and one refusal sentence when it cannot fit. That is this lab's placement problem solved
   by someone else's tool, and its first-day bug ("the fit pricing only priced all-weights-on-GPU, so a
   125B MoE came up at a 4,096 window") is the same failure our dense-27B test found by hand.
   `status: evidence-primary`.

## 3. Strata: three independent measurements, and one of them changes text

**No release since v0.1.45** (2026-10-17T06:53:17Z); no PR merged between 2026-10-17T14:00Z and now.
What moved is the record of what 0.1.45 does.

### 3a. The decode regression is real, older than 0.1.45, and bisected (#2936)

Single **RTX 3090 24 GB**, 88 GB RAM, expert arena fully pinned (**zero disk reads**), Xeon E5-2680 v4
(AVX2, no AVX-512), CUDA 13.0, gcc 13.3, **the same llama.cpp submodule commit for all four arms**,
Qwen3.8-Flash-Next GSQ-RCO IQ3_S, 262K context, KV int8, `--spec 4 --mtp rt --kv-resident 32768`.
Bench: same prompts, 5 runs × 3 warmups, engine swapped per arm, ready-poll and smoke each time.

| arm | Strata | build pins | narrative decode | code decode | CPU pool / expert | GPU W |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A  | 0.1.43 | off/off (production) | 102.6 | 118.4 | 82 µs | 182 |
| A' | 0.1.43 retest | off/off | 103.2 | 115.9 | 82 µs | - |
| B  | 0.1.45 | on/on (release pins) | **78.6 (−23 %)** | **96.6 (−18 %)** | 96-102 µs | 166 |
| D  | 0.1.45 | off/off (rebuilt to match A) | **79.4 (−23 %)** | **86.3 (−27 %)** | 82-85 µs | - |

A vs A' agree within 1 %, so positional/thermal bias is out. Ruled out with numbers: **build pins**
(arm D compiles 0.1.45 with the same flags as the 0.1.43 production binary and still regresses) and
**the CPU expert pool / PCIe** (arm D's per-expert cost is 82-85 µs against 0.1.43's 82 µs, and the
host→device probe reads 11.3 GB/s on every arm). An added **0.1.44.1** arm on the same rig gives
96.99 / 109.25 tok/s on the clean re-run, i.e. **−6 %/−8 % versus 0.1.43, so the regression starts at
0.1.44**, and 0.1.45's −23 %/−27 % is additive on top of it. Corroborating shape: **GPU power 182 → 166 W
while host CPU busy rises 50.2 % → 56.9 %** for the same token count - the GPU is waiting, not computing
slower. Suspects named are all decode-scheduling: the 0.1.44 "pipelined-decode and latency work",
`STRATA_ROUTE_TAIL_SKIP` interacting with the newer scheduler, the MTP router guard under `--mtp rt`,
and `pcie_frac` self-adapting to a different point (0.20 vs 0.20-0.25) at equal measured pool speed.
**Applies to this box: probably, via the same code path, not via the same geometry** - a 3090 with the
arena pinned is not a 12 GB card streaming experts, and our pins are 0.1.39-0.1.41, so nothing we have
recorded is affected; the point is prospective. `status: evidence-primary`.

### 3b. `presence_penalty: 1.5` reaches temperature-0 non-thinking requests (#2940)

Introduced by 0.1.45 (`a8091f4d`, #2685). A request with thinking off picks up the model card's
instruct sampling set, so **`presence_penalty: 1.5` lands on every non-thinking request that does not
send one** - including `temperature: 0`, which the reporter reasonably expected to be plain greedy - and
a config `sampling` block that sets temperature/top_p/top_k still inherits the card's presence penalty.
Measured **~89 → 65-72 tok/s** on 4× 7900 XT (UD-Q4_K_XL, layer split 12,24,39, `--pipeline-windows 2`,
MTP).

Three follow-ups make it sharper, and all three are for us:

- **It is server-side, not engine-side** (beastrobin, 2026-10-18T04:25Z, reproduced on **Windows + RTX
  PRO 6000 Blackwell, sm_120**): `_run` merges `{**model_card_sampling(...), **self.sampling_defaults,
  **self.shared}` at `serve/server.py:4193`, so only fields the *request* sends override. That matches
  the reporter's own note that a 0.1.44 engine under a 0.1.45 server was slow.
- **Any repetition penalty also disables `--pipeline-windows`** for that request (Vop3p,
  2026-10-18T07:07Z): `pl_serial` sends a request with `hist_n > 0` to the serial loop and says so
  **once per process**, so the message is easy to miss. That is a second mechanism producing the same
  symptom.
- **`--pipeline-windows` is not the only thing it changes.** The consequence we care about is the
  answer: a silent `presence_penalty` at temperature 0 is precisely the kind of hidden default that makes
  two runs disagree while every declared flag matches. It sits in the same bucket as **#1928** (greedy
  depends on a timing EMA) and it is worse, because it is a merge-order bug rather than a documented
  trade-off.

**Applies to this box: not to any recorded number** - the change entered in 0.1.45, and our Strata A/B
ladder (0.1.39 → 0.1.40.3 → 0.1.41) stops before it. It applies to any future bump, and to the general
rule that a server's default sampling block is part of the experiment declaration.
`status: evidence-primary` for the mechanism, `lead` for the vendor's intent.

### 3c. A retraction, kept because the retraction is the finding (in #2940)

cybernetic-ua (2026-10-18T09:27Z) reported a **second** server-side slowdown path, having pinned
`presence_penalty: 0` explicitly - then **retracted it** at 12:33Z: *"It was a measurement artifact on our
side, not a Strata regression."* Their rig ran the chat agent on **the same model the bench replayed**,
so during night runs *"every agent turn reloaded a second 44 GB engine into RAM mid-bench, and our
watchdog failed to kill it (a pid-namespace mismatch on MSYS)"*. Once the harness identified the bench
ancestor by `CommandLine`, **every `serve/` commit between v0.1.44.1 and v0.1.45 measured 42-52 tok/s,
identical to 0.1.44.1**.

That is this lab's own "one inference workload at a time" rule violated by someone else, caught, and
published - and the false conclusion it produced (a whole second regression path) is exactly the size of
error the rule prevents. Two lab-relevant details: the artifact was **a second process holding the model**,
which is the state our gates watch, and their detector failed for **a pid-namespace reason**, which is
the class of bug our own earlier `sys.path` incident belongs to (*name the thing you mean; do not let the
environment pick it for you*).

### 3d. On an architecture the release does not claim, the answers differ (#2932)

2× Radeon Pro VII 16 GB (gfx906), 64 GB RAM, Ubuntu 24.04, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next GSQ-RCO **IQ3_S native
pack**, `--kv-resident 32768`, MTP spec 4, `STRATA_ROUTE_TAIL_SKIP=0` on both arms. Interleaved
old/new/old/new, fixed-nonce bench + 6 greedy prompts × 768 tokens × 2:

| | prompt 4k / 32k tok/s | decode 4k / 32k | decode bench | hit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1.43.1 (two arms) | 395 / 557, 393 / 551 | 43.1 / 51.3, 42.9 / 51.3 | 44.3, 44.7 | 89.5 % |
| 0.1.45 (two arms) | 394 / 564, 393 / 557 | 43.9 / **44.8**, 43.7 / **44.8** | 43.7, 44.0 | 89.9 % |

- **Each version reproduces itself exactly, arm to arm, and 12 of 12 greedy decode-bench texts differ
  between the versions, from character 9-259.** The release notes say Flash-Next answers are
  byte-identical, and the architectures they gate on are **sm_60 / sm_86 / HIP gfx1201 / A750 / M4**
  (yesterday's reading of the release text; the reporter paraphrases them as "RTX 3060 / P100 / R9700 /
  A750"). **sm_120 and gfx906 are both outside the claim**, and 0.1.45's own coverage note says the RTX
  5070 was not run. This is the first *measured* counterexample to the identity claim, on a card the
  vendor excluded.
- 32k decode is ~13 % slower (51.3 → 44.8), and the 256-token reply takes 6258 ms against 5227 ms with
  **229 vs 208 verify windows** (drafts accepted 139 vs 162).
- `STRATA_TOPK_OLD=1` on 0.1.45 gives a **third** set of texts, matching neither version, although
  #1749's docs say the wide and original top-k give the same ids.
- Separate good news: `--pipeline-windows 2` with `STRATA_ARENA_MMAP=1` **no longer stalls** on 0.1.45
  (it stalled on 0.1.41/0.1.43.1 with "verify window (pipelined): the CPU experts of layer 22"), though
  it gave no decode gain on the mmap arena (43.0 vs 44.0).

**Why this earns more than a line:** our determinism notes (2026-10-11 "the decision endpoint is
deterministic", "three byte-identical probes") are bounded by #1928 on paper; #2932 is the same boundary
demonstrated between engine versions on an unclaimed architecture. Any future "same flags, same answer"
claim on this box must declare the engine version *and* treat the vendor's identity list as evidence
about other cards, not this one.

### 3e. Two leads with numbers, both in our geometry class

- **#3059** (created 2026-10-18T14:08Z, 0 comments): **Windows file tier - reading experts through the
  file cache after load gave +26 % decode** on an RX 7800 XT 16 GB / 64 GB RAM / Windows 11 box, with a
  60,047-token fresh prompt (0 reused), three runs per arm, **only one switch changed**, same binary,
  same day, `STRATA_UNBUFFERED_LOAD=1` in both arms:

  | | prompt tok/s | decode tok/s | TTFT s | total s |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | `STRATA_R6_FILE_READ_CACHE=0` | 1,399.8 | 28.5 (24.6-28.9) | 43.08 | 56.19 |
  | `STRATA_R6_FILE_READ_CACHE=1` | 1,476.4 | **36.0 (32.2-36.3)** | 40.74 | 51.20 |
  | change | +5.5 % | **+26.3 %** | −5.4 % | −8.9 % |

  **The generated text was identical pair by pair** (345/381/340 tokens), and physical SSD reads fell
  947.9 → 306.7 MiB/s during the prompt and 678.4 → **24.7** MiB/s during decode. The mechanism as
  described: with `STRATA_UNBUFFERED_LOAD=1` every expert outside RAM and VRAM is read with
  `FILE_FLAG_NO_BUFFERING` once `drop_mapping()` closes the views; the proposed change keeps the
  unbuffered handles for the initial load and then reopens `experts.bin`/the GGUF shards with
  `FILE_FLAG_OVERLAPPED` only, so later reads can be served by the Windows file cache.
  **Applies: yes, directly** - 64 GB RAM, Windows 11, 12 GB card, a 60k-token prompt is our
  `--max-context 65536` configuration, and this lab recorded on 2026-10-06 that a verified minimal-startup
  reboot (free physical 15.14 → 18.19 GiB) *"was absorbed by the engine's file cache"*. That observation
  now has a mechanism and an opt-in switch. **Read the cost honestly:** it is an **offered patch in an
  issue**, not a documented flag, and there is no Strata release after 0.1.45 - so testing it means a
  build, which is the same decision class as any engine bump. `status: lead` (single user, single box,
  but a controlled A/B with identical text).
- **#3056** (created 2026-10-18T14:05Z): 2× RTX 2080 Ti (sm_75), `STRATA_SM75_PERF=1` **verified
  bitwise**, and **prefill +46 % from 0.1.43.1 to 0.1.45**. Prefill, not decode, and sm_75 - recorded
  because it is the counterweight to #2936: 0.1.45 is not uniformly slower, the direction depends on
  what the release optimised.

The item yesterday's shortlist ranked sixth was **"price our own GGUF on Colibri natively, before any
scoring"**. **#2189** is someone else doing it, on Windows, with a `qwen4exp` GGUF, and the maintainer's
reply turns it from a verdict into a **quantisation-dependent** one:

- **The two import bugs are Windows bugs and are being fixed in #2197:** `gguf_import.py` writes JSON
  with `Path.write_text()` under the locale encoding, so cp1252 raises `UnicodeEncodeError` on a
  character at position 6744 (workaround `PYTHONUTF8=1`); and an **absolute** gguf path in `gguf.json`
  is joined onto the model directory, producing `D:\colibri\models\flashnext/D:\...` and an engine exit
  (workaround: a junction plus a relative path). The maintainer confirms UTF-8 everywhere, absolute paths
  kept as written (`D:\`, `D:/`, `\\host\share\`), and `coli run` working for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next.
- **The speed split is per-kernel.** The reporter's file has **IQ2_S** gate/up experts (47 layers, one
  IQ3_S) and **IQ4_NL** down, trunk Q6_K/Q8_0. On an x86-64-v3 build: **IQ4_NL and Q6_K go through the
  16-row interleaved kernels at ~80-87 GB/s on 16 Zen 4 cores; IQ2_S goes through a per-row kernel at
  ~16 GB/s.** #2198 lifts IQ2_S 2.59 → 3.19 and IQ3_S 2.21 → 4.26 GB/s per core, bit-identical.
- **Maintainer's reference point, which is the number that matters:** a desktop **i5-13500 (6 P + 8 E),
  CPU only, Qwen3.8 IQ2_XS (also IQ2_S experts) does 9.0-9.4 tok/s decode and ~28 tok/s prefill** - so
  the reporter's 1.5-1.9 tok/s is *several times below what these kernels can explain*, and the
  maintainer says so rather than declaring victory. Asked and answered: no fast path is being missed
  (`Q38_MOE_GROUPED`/`Q38_TRUNK_CPU_INT8` do not change expert kernels for that file); expert conversion
  at import is **not** available for qwen4exp (the engine reads it in place); and there is **no
  compatibility path** - GGUF is meant to be first-class on CPU, and the IQ2_S kernel is the known gap.
- **What this does and does not say about ours.** Our reference pack is **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B Q4_K_M**, and
  **Q4_K is not named anywhere in that reply** - the fast-path list is IQ4_NL and Q6_K. So the honest
  reading is: the reported 5x is IQ2_S-specific and does not transfer by name, and **whether our Q4_K
  experts take the interleaved kernel is unverified**. That is now a question to ask, not a probe to run.
- Also open and worth one line: **#2196** SSE streams cut at exactly ~60 s for okhttp-based Java clients

## 7. Research: the index has not moved, and two papers in the 10-15 batch bear on our instruments

**arXiv's index for this environment still reaches 2026-10-15** - nothing submitted on 10-16/17/18 is
queryable yet, checked today against the API (newest cs.CL submission published 2026-10-15). So there
is **no new paper to report**, and re-listing yesterday's four would be the re-coverage the standing
rule forbids. What today's query did surface are two 10-15 papers yesterday's file did not read, and
both speak to instruments this lab runs:

- **2610.22244, "Language Is a Security Surface for Agent Guards"** (2026-10-15). Step-level guards
  decide before each tool call whether the next action is safe. Built **LinguaGuardBench** from the
  **1,220 labelled steps** of TS-Bench AgentDojo-Traj by translating **only the user instruction** into
  German, French, Turkish, Swahili and Amharic while keeping every tool call, observation and label
  **byte-identical**, so any verdict change is attributable to language alone, with six English
  rewordings and a whitespace placebo as controls. Finding: small purpose-built guards change verdicts
  beyond their own noise floor and **the effect grows with language rarity** - among nine guards under
  13B, the placebo-adjusted flip rate rises from **+1.5 pp on French to +4.4 pp on Amharic**; 12-30B
  open guards block more benign steps (**up to +5.0 pp false positives**), every significant recall loss
  is below 10B (**up to −7.4 pp**), frontier guards shift by about a point at most. `status:
  evidence-primary`. - **Applies as a method, not a result:** it is a controlled demonstration that
  **perturbing one field and holding the rest byte-identical** isolates a cause, which is exactly the
  design our 18-window decision screen uses (and exactly what the 2026-09-25 run got wrong when its
  "46 synthetic probabilities" turned out not to be 46 independent cases). The result itself does not
  transfer: our screen is English, and the guards that moved are under 30B.
- **2610.22187, "Change the Number, Change the Answer: Assessing Rating Scale Sensitivity in LLM-Based
  Text Scoring"** (2026-10-15). Defines **scale sensitivity** - variation in LLM-assigned scores caused
  by rating-scale design - split into **numerical** (normalized scores move) and **semantic** (score
  interpretation moves), and crosses **2 formats × 4 ranges (3, 5, 11, 21 points) × 2 signs** across
  three tasks. `status: evidence-primary`. - **Applies directly to a reading we already published:** our
  decision suite's weakest primitive is `score` (macro 52.6 nimble, 50.4 kev4, 31.9 lev, 33.0 kev0, 28.7
  laya), and the one failure we localised was **lev under-predicting the top of a 5-point scale**
  (summeval-consistency 2.54 mean expected against 3.61 mean target, 121 of 144 targets at level 4).
  A paper that shows scale design alone moves scores is the confound we did not control, and the next
  comparison across model families on that subset should at least declare the scale it was scored on.
- Nothing else in the 10-15 batch touches an instrument we own; **DropKV**, the remote-KV warning and
  the two eval designs were covered yesterday and their state has not changed.
