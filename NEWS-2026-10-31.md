### 2.4 [GH][VRAM] The resident budget is one allocation on Windows, and past the driver's limit it silently becomes pageable

**#4808** (2026-10-31T12:21Z, a PR by a contributor, validated on Windows 11 26200, driver 621.01, RTX 3090 24 GB +
RTX 3080 10 GB, 64 GB DDR4, Qwen3.8 IQ3_S with MTP, `--resident-budget-gib 35`):

- *"On Windows the RAM-budget complement (`--resident-budget-gib N`) is refused by `cudaHostAlloc` as one block once it
  passes the driver's single-allocation limit, so it falls back to pageable memory."*
- `STRATA_PARTIAL_PIN=1` helps but **also registers one block**, stepping down 2 GiB at a time - *"on this PC that stops
  at ~20 GiB of a 34.7 GiB complement, while the expert arena on the same PC pins 41 GiB in 43 slices of 1 GiB."*
- With **`STRATA_PARTIAL_PIN_SLICED=1`** the hottest prefix is registered slice by slice at an expert boundary:
  **all 34.73 GiB in 35 slices**. Off by default; without the variable the code path is byte-for-byte the existing one.
- The WDDM detail that makes this a Windows-only problem: *"under WDDM, `cudaHostGetDevicePointer` on a registered
  slice does not return its host address"*, so each slice keeps its own device alias and `FileExpertSource::device_alias()`
  selects the blob's slice with an `upper_bound`.

**Why this is on our list rather than the vendor's.** Our 2026-10-27 ladder found `--resident-budget-gib 16` was
**granted 5.29-10.18 GiB** and that *"the metric that does move is the granted resident budget, not the requested one"*.
`#4808` supplies a mechanism of exactly that shape: a single-block registration that steps down until the driver accepts
it, i.e. **a declared number that is granted at a smaller number, with no line in the log that says so**. The switch is
unmerged, so 0.1.55 does not have it; what we can do today is re-read our 21 arm JSONs for the *shape* and name the
candidate cause (shortlist item 4).

### 3.1 [GH][ENGINE] `draft-dflash`/`draft-dspark` break greedy identity on qwen35moe at 100 % acceptance

**#31472** (opened 2026-10-31T12:58Z, 1 comment): at `--temp 0`, speculative decoding with `--spec-type draft-dflash` or
`draft-dspark` is **not token-identical** to autoregressive decoding of the same target, same prompt, same seed. The
decisive cell: **at `p_min=0.90`, 184 of 184 drafts accepted (mean length 5.28), zero rejects - and the output still
differs by 8 lines.** Since `common_sampler_sample_and_accept_n` (`common/sampling.cpp:690`) accepts a draft only when
it equals the target sampler's own output, *"every emitted token was target-sampled from a multi-token verify batch, so
the target's verify-pass logits differ from its single-step AR logits (batch-shape numerics), or the spec-path
recurrent-state handling (GDN) diverges on accept."*

The reporter's ladder is the useful part, because it kills the obvious tuning answer: acceptance rises **monotonically**
0.382 → 0.537 → 0.849 → 0.934 → 1.000 while fidelity is **non-monotonic** (one prompt identical at 0.00, differing at
0.30-0.90, identical at 1.00 - where no drafts are made at all). *"No usable knee: any engaged draft diverges
unpredictably."* And the secondary line: **spec was not faster than AR on the CPU arms** (2.6-7.4 vs 4.9-9.0 tok/s).

Classified as a **duplicate of #25618** by a maintainer (2026-10-31T14:06Z), which is the older and broader report:
**it needs a quantised target** (Q4_K_M diverges, bf16 matches 4/4, `ngram-simple`/`ngram-mod` match on the same
quantised target), it is **not DSpark-only** (Gemma-4 + MTP fails too), and one contributor reproduced it on
**RTX 5080 Laptop 16 GB, sm_120, b11513 with `-DGGML_CUDA_FA_QUANTS=all`, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B UD-Q4_K_M, `-ncmoe 21`** -
*"all 3 answers differ from control for both MTP (n-max 3) and DFlash2 (n-max 7)"* - and named a fix branch
(`Apoze` branch `fix/cuda-speculative-batch-invariance`, commit `626344551`), adding that *"the MoE model (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B)
needed two more changes"*.

**Why it lands on us.** Our 2026-10-08 MTP row was **+31.2 % per token, -7.3 % wall, accuracy unchanged**, measured by
comparing *answer letters* on the coding batteries. Letters are exactly the instrument that cannot see this: the

### 9.1 [ARX][ENGINE] 2610.40287 - speculation's energy saving is bounded by accepted tokens

*"Only Accepted Tokens Count: An Energy Accounting of Speculative Decoding Under Latency Targets"* (published
2026-10-29). The mechanism: *"During decode, every weight is read from memory for every generated token. Batching
amortizes this read across users but makes no individual user faster. Speculative decoding does: a drafter proposes
several future tokens, and the target model verifies them in a single forward pass that reuses the same weight read.
Because every drafted token must be verified whether or not it is accepted, the energy speculation saves depends on how
many drafted tokens are accepted."* And the honest limit they name: *"This saving is hard to pin down. It varies across
target models from different attention famil[ies]..."*

That is the energy form of a result we already have in wall-clock: our 2026-10-08 MTP arm was **+31.2 % per token,
-7.3 % wall, +21.7 % tokens** - i.e. the verification passes were paid and the acceptance did not cover them. Today's
llama.cpp threads make it operational: acceptance is printed by the engine, and **acceptance can move decode by 5.5 %
at fixed flags** (sec. 2.2). So the missing column in every speculative row we hold is **accepted/offered**, and the
second missing column is **tokens generated** - the two multipliers that turn a per-token rate into a wall rate.

### 9.2 [ARX][ENGINE] 2610.39442 - softmax routing concentrates, and concentration is a barrier, not a preference

*"Softmax Routing Dynamics in Mixture-of-Experts Models: Collapse and Recovery"* (2026-10-29). From the abstract: *"Softmax
routing ... can concentrate probability on a subset of experts, suppressing access to the others"*; for fixed experts *"a
stationary-loss barrier identifies the face supporting the optimal routed output"*, and *"every fixed positive adaptation
rate yields an interior routing limit"*.

This is the theory for what we measured on 2026-10-28 and what the vendor reverted on 2026-10-29: a bonus on the experts
that happen to be resident **concentrates routing** and *suppresses* the rest, which is what a 22.9 % perplexity rise at
prior 1.5 looks like, and what the vendor called *"repeated text in long sessions"*. The paper's useful addition is that
the suppression is a **barrier**, not a slope: raising the prior past it does not buy back the suppressed experts, and
that is consistent with the flat prior's own KL curve (0.075 → 0.094 → 0.225 for 0.5 → 0.7 → 1.5, sec. 2.5).

### 9.3 [ARX][VRAM] 2610.38277 - precision reduction *accelerates* delayed loss spikes, and the site is the LM head

*"The Role of Quantization in Loss Spikes"* (2026-10-28): *"We study a particularly costly form of this instability
associated with output logit growth: delayed, terminal divergences that emerge only after prolonged training, which we
call time-bomb spikes. We find that these instabilities are preceded by rapidly growing quantization error in the LM
head ... reducing numerical precision accelerates time-bomb spikes, while increasing precision improves stability."*

That is a training result, and we do not train. It is kept because it names the **LM head** as the site where precision
loss shows first, and because yesterday's pack audit (2609.05881) and today's granite NVFP4 report (#31449/#31448) are
both about arithmetic that is *silently* wrong rather than loudly broken.

### 9.4 [ARX] Two more, one line each

- **2610.40541** *"On the Fundamental Limits of Prefill-Based Decoding Length Prediction"*: benchmarks *"conflate predictor
  quality with task hardness"* and the instability traces to *"the inherent stochasticity of autoregressive decoding"* -
  the per-step stopping probability *"ramps up over hundreds of steps"*. Relevant to anyone quoting "predicted output
  length"; our tokens-per-task column is a measured count, not a prediction, so nothing changes.
- **2606.24033 v2** *"Block-GTQ: RoPE-Block Rate Allocation for KV-Cache Quantization"* (v2 updated 2026-10-29): allocates
  cached-key precision by **RoPE frequency block** with a label-free query-key energy score. Directly on the `k8v4` axis
  from 2026-10-25: it says a uniform K/V bit-width is the wrong parameterisation, and the block is the unit.
