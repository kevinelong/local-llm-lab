# News scan, 2026-10-17: Kolibri-1 left the fork, and Strata 0.1.45 landed with regressions

Scope: what moved since `NEWS-2026-10-16.md`, in the engines we run and their neighbours, the model
packs, the literature, the related projects and the release feeds. **Reading only.** Nothing was
downloaded, installed, built or run; no engine was started; no gate was checked; no inference and no
benchmark were attempted; and **nothing under `C:/Users/kevin/ai_bench` was read or touched** - the
Colibri knowledge comparison owns the inference slot. Every claim carries a source and a date;
second-hand material is labelled, and numbers that could not be verified from their source are
called unverified rather than repeated. Everything here is a lead, not evidence, until measured on
this box.

Baseline discipline, as the standing rule requires: a release, pack or paper already covered earns a
line only when its **state** changes. Where that happened, this file says what changed; where a
yesterday item moved, it says which one, including one **correction of yesterday's "nothing moved"
list** (section 2).

## 1. Three state changes, and the decision each one forces

1. **llama.cpp now supports Kolibri-1 upstream** - PR **#30673 merged 2026-10-17T11:00:18Z**
   (`https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/30673`, opened 2026-10-16T13:12:46Z). Every Kolibri-1
   number this lab has recorded came from the community `hob-b11434` build, because stock llama.cpp
   had no `kolibri1` support. That dependency has an upstream replacement as of today.
   `status: evidence-primary`.
2. **Strata v0.1.45 shipped 2026-10-17T06:53:17Z** and within eight hours had **three regression
   reports**, two of them on small-VRAM machines and one on Windows with our reference family. This
   is the release that was supposed to make "the reference model on the second engine" cheaper; it
   currently makes it harder. `status: evidence-primary` (release) + `lead` (the reports are single-user
   accounts, but they are dated today and none has been answered yet).
3. **Ollama v0.40.6 exists as a prerelease** (published 2026-10-16T22:00:46Z, `prerelease: true`) and
   it contains two things on our path: **`gguf: add the q2_0 tensor and file types` (#18967)** and
   **`win: prevent orphaned runners` (#18500)**. A prerelease is not a pin, but the orphan fix names a
   failure mode this lab has already paid for (six orphaned `llama-server` processes on 2026-10-09,
   ~8.9 GiB held). `status: evidence-primary`.

## 2. llama.cpp: sixteen merges in a day, three of them ours

Latest tag **b11712** (published 2026-10-17T14:01:29Z, target commit `10b8b755`). Sixteen PRs merged
since 2026-10-16T11:00Z; three bear on this box.

## 3. Strata 0.1.45: a real release, and a reason not to bump yet

**v0.1.45, published 2026-10-17T06:53:17Z** (created 06:50:44Z; assets: `strata-windows-x64.zip`
165,243,142 B, `-cuda12` 242,782,835 B, `-hip` 643,211,025 B, all with `sha256` digests in the API).

### 3a. What it changes that touches our geometry

- **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B got faster and gained vision.** New decode kernels with the old kernels' bits
  (18,360 comparisons, 0 mismatches), a prompt delta rule that loads one token ahead, and a **bigger
  prompt chunk by default - 4096 / 2048 / 1024 tokens, whatever fits** (`--prefill 512` restores the
  old one). Vendor numbers: R9700 32 GB UD-Q4_K_XL decode **83.2 -> 117.8 tok/s (1.42x, 5 of 5
  pairs)**, 4K prompt **3,453 -> 6,220 (1.80x)**; with `--mtp 2` 167.7 -> 183.7. **RTX 3060 12 GB in
  hybrid mode (the CPU-bound case, like ours): decode +2%, prompt +3%.** `status: vendor claim`.
- **The chunk change is an output change, and the release says so.** *"Text from the old chunk size
  differs a little on long prompts (mean KL 0.004 against chunk 512, the same size as the llama.cpp
  CPU-GPU floor); prompts shorter than the chunk are unchanged."* Any A/B against an old chunk size is
  therefore a text A/B, not just a speed A/B - the same trap our own placement work already
  documented (prose moves, letters do not) and the reason to score answers, not strings.
- **`--parallel N` (up to 8) is not for us, and the release prices it against us.** Every window
  carries one token of each conversation so shared weights and experts are read once: on an R9700 with
  every expert in VRAM, 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 requests 83.9 / 173.5 / 252 / 325 tok/s against llama.cpp's
  73.8 / 107 / 165 / 208. **On a card that cannot hold every expert the slots take VRAM from the
  expert cache: a request alone runs 4-11% slower**, and next to one request at a time *with* the draft
  layer, N together are only 1.0-1.15x faster (RTX 3060 12 GB at 8K, 4 requests: 1.15x, alone -4%;
  RTX A4000 / Tesla P100 16 GB at 32K, 2 requests: 1.0x, alone -7 to -11%). Setup's own sizing: **12 GB:
  4 slots at 8K, none at 32K**. This lab runs one agent at a time at 65,536 context, so the correct
  setting is and remains **one** - and this is the first release that states the cost in numbers.
- **A resident-RAM copy keeps the prompt path's lend region (#1896, elightcap)** - with room in the RAM
  budget, decode **+7%** and prompt reading **+2.5%** on an RTX 3060, 5 of 5 pairs. That is our
  geometry (12 GB card, 64 GB RAM, a declared 12 GiB resident budget). `status: vendor claim`.
- **A do-not-combine, and it names our own arm:** `STRATA_EXCHANGE_ROTATE=1` (+5% decode on a 3060
  with the resident RAM copy) - *"do not use it with the resident copy under `--expert-cache 1500` yet:
  the engine did not start on one 3060 box"*. Our arm B **was** `--expert-cache 1500`. `status: lead`.
- **The identity claim does not cover our card.** *"Answers of the Qwen3.8-Flash-Next models are the
  same as in 0.1.44.1 (byte-identical in our identity tests on every GPU machine we tested: CUDA sm_60
  / sm_86, HIP gfx1201, Intel A750, Mac M4)"* - **sm_120 is absent from that list**, and the release's
  own coverage note says *"Not run: Windows hardware ..., Intel Arc ..., the RTX 5070."* So the
  byte-identity promise for our pack family is verified on architectures that are not this one.
- Smaller items with our names on them: **#2396** (a converter's all-zero `compress_ratios` list is now
  treated as absent - 0.1.44 refused **OrcaRouter's Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-Uncensored** GGUFs, which is the
  family several of the third-party Strata packs in section 6 are built on); **#2346** UD-Q5_K_XL size
  check; **#2403** `--kv q4_0` with KV streaming and MTP faulted on a Windows RX 6900 XT and the fix is
  a batched verify window with *"identical tokens on three GPUs"*, with the honest caveat *"We do not
  know that this fixes the fault; the reporter's retest will tell"*; **#2406** on cards under 24 GB
  "images: yes" now gives the **CPU encoder** (4-6 s per picture, up to 300 tokens), because the GPU
  encoder holds ~1.4 GB of VRAM away from the experts.

### 3b. Three reports filed against it today, one of them ours to care about

- **#2894** (open, 2026-10-17T13:11:48Z, 0 comments): **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B setup fails on Windows with the
  prebuilt 0.1.45 engine.** The log is explicit - `[ok] ready-made engine 0.1.45 for sm_75, sm_86,
  sm_89, sm_120 (CUDA 13.0)` followed by `[X] this engine (0.1.45) does not run Qwen3.6-35B-A3B yet -
  update Strata, or compile the engine with --build, and run setup again`. The reporter's card is an
  **RTX 5070 Ti (sm_120)** - i.e. the prebuilt advertises the arch and still refuses the family.
  **Consequence for us: "put the reference model on the second engine" is blocked on Windows prebuilt

- **#2856** (open, 2026-10-17T07:35:14Z, 2 comments): **"Significant regression after 0.1.45 on 32 GB
  RAM + 6 GB VRAM system (previously working config)"** - IQ3_XXS, `--expert-cache auto`,
  `--max-context 81920`, `k8v4` KV, `--expert-cache`/`--adapt-*` tuning that *"worked while having 41
  experts on GPU, around 13 token/s decode, so quite usable"*. A 32 GB / 6 GB box is the nearest

- **#2897** (open, 2026-10-17T13:30:13Z, 1 comment): **"After updating to 0.1.45 I face out of memory!"**
  - a 313k-context IQ3_S config that previously ran now dies; the log shows `experts loaded: 46.84 GiB

- **And an older Windows/NVIDIA regression that 0.1.45 does not claim to fix:** **#2453** (open since
  2026-10-14, 7 comments, updated today) - RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB, 128 GB RAM, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next IQ3_S,
  64K, int8 KV, images off, setup's own tuning: **v0.1.42 69.2 tok/s** (`expert cache 4269 slots,
  8.14 GiB of VRAM`, `decode expert cache hit rate 91.1%`, `KV streaming 99.93%`, RAM ~64 GB) against
  **v0.1.44.1 55-58.8 tok/s, repeatable** (RAM ~68 GB). That is a **16-20% Windows/NVIDIA decode
  regression between 0.1.42 and 0.1.44.1**, on the same class of card as ours. It is the strongest
  available argument that **bumping Strata for speed is the wrong direction on Windows**, and it puts
  a boundary on our own 2026-10-08 conclusion that "the engine bump is speed-neutral" - that covered
  0.1.39 -> 0.1.41, and the ladder has moved since.

### 3c. An open issue that explains a boundary on our own determinism claims

**#1928** (open since 2026-10-11, updated 2026-10-17T12:21:29Z): **"Greedy output depends on
wall-clock timing"** - on a native IQ pack at `--greedy` the same prompt produces different token
streams from one fresh engine to the next, because two documented rules combine: `DraftPolicy::choose`
picks, round by round, whether to verify a prompt-lookup suffix window instead of the MTP window, from
the **EMA of measured round times** (`src/spec/draft_policy.cpp:60-89`, cost EMA `:91-97`,
`kCostAlpha` `:13`; the suffix drafter is on by default, `suffix_draft = 3`, `generate.cpp:652`); and
a CPU expert is computed with the single-token dot product for one token but the multi-token kernels
for two or more, **which round differently** (`src/kernels/cpu/native_expert.cpp:115-131`). So how
many tokens in a window share an expert changes the logits, and the window is chosen from a timing
EMA. `status: evidence-primary` (line-referenced).
- **Why this is worth the line:** this lab's 2026-10-11 determinism probe concluded "the decision
  endpoint is deterministic" and later "this engine is deterministic for this prompt and length (three
  byte-identical probes)". Both remain true as measured, and both are now bounded: determinism holds
  when the suffix drafter is not reshaping windows, and wall-clock noise is an input, not an
  irrelevance. Any future A/B whose words "same flags, same answer" rests on needs its drafter state
  declared.

### 3d. One issue whose measurement is about our own loop class

**#2901** (open, 2026-10-17T14:02:44Z): **`STRATA_LOOP_BREAK=1`** (opt-in) pushes a `--serve` reply out
of a span loop - break mode when a span has been written three times (`run[p] >= 2p` and
`run[p] >= STRATA_LOOP_BREAK_MIN`, default 256 tokens), for `STRATA_LOOP_BREAK_SPAN` (default 1024)
tokens, ended early by a think/tool-call/code-fence token. The motivating measurement is the part to
keep: **across 4.06M tokens of one user's opencode sessions (Swift 1.5 IQ3_XXS on a 12 GB laptop), 11%
of the sessions longer than 3K tokens had a hard loop and 5.7% of all tokens were repetition; one regex
task ran a 29K-token loop of the same paragraph.** `status: lead` (one user, one machine) - but it is
the first external count of a rate this lab has only ever recorded as shapes (a sentence repeated
3,043 times; truncation inside thinking; the ledger's early termination). Note the tradeoff: it
changes generated text, so it is an output-affecting knob and cannot be on during a scored run.

### 3e. A supply-chain hazard for any setup run

**#2902** (open, 2026-10-17T14:06:07Z): **"setup: SHA-256 check is wrong for large shards and deletes
correct files."** Read it before the next `setup.py` invocation on a multi-shard pack: a checksum bug
that deletes files it has just verified is worse than one that stops. `status: lead`. (This lab's own
rule already prefers serving a staged directory over re-running setup - the 2026-10-12 eight-hour
deadlock - so the practical exposure is small.)

## 7. Research: one engine warning with numbers, one method note, two eval designs

arXiv's index for this environment reaches **2026-10-15**; nothing submitted on 10-16/17 is
queryable yet, so this section is one day older than the rest of the file.

- **Remote KV tiers can be slower than recompute** - **2610.21500** (2026-10-15), "When the KV Cache Is
  Slower Than the GPU": a disaggregated deployment of GLM-5.3 (FP8 MoE with MLA) on 8xB300 nodes with
  prefill context parallelism, backed by LMCache over a Valkey store. Enabling the remote tier left
  **median TTFT unchanged but raised the 16k-token p99 from 3.96 s to 17.3 s**, and on an isolated
  replay **a remote hit took 0.88 s against 0.31 s to recompute the prompt**. `status: evidence-primary`.
  - Applies here as a *warning*, not a lever: **Strata issue #2734** (2026-10-16) is a request to
    integrate LMCache, and this paper is the measurement that would make us refuse it without a
    tail-latency gate. It also matches two of our own negatives - a warm cache that did not help
    (`strata-warmcache-20261011`), and MTP buying throughput while costing wall time.
- **Fidelity is not usability** - **2610.22016** (2026-10-15), "Close Enough is Nowhere Near": a frozen
  transformer only reads caches close to the one it would have written itself (a "tolerable set"),
  geometric similarity to that cache **does not predict** usability, and **the failure is not gradual**,
  so a more faithful substitute is not the remedy. `status: evidence-primary`. Method note for us: this
  is the cross-model analogue of our own measured asymmetry - **placement moves 65/93 justifications
  and 0/93 letters** - and it is the reason "closer to the reference" is not a scoring argument.
- **Two eval designs, not adoptable today:** **2610.21109 Kolab** (2026-10-15) - 115+ hours of screen
  recordings, 50 professionals from 40 occupations, long-horizon human-agent collaboration, motivated by
  the claim that strong automation scores do not imply collaboration; **2610.22350 FDE-Gym**
  (2026-10-15) - 95 end-to-end delivery tasks from 20 customer scenarios in 18 sectors, scored by
  improvement in the customer's own success metric, with scripted stakeholders. Both argue that
  `repo`/`ledger`-style check-counting measures the wrong half of delivery. `status: evidence-primary`.
- One engine-side paper, recorded and not queued: **2610.22130 DropKV** (2026-10-15) decouples
  residual-output perturbation into independent per-token scores to make KV eviction both principled and
  kernelisable. Needs engine work; the same bucket as yesterday's KV papers.
