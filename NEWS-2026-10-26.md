### 3.6 [ENGINE] #4206: `--prompt-cache-every` is a no-op below the chunk size, and the deepest checkpoint is moved by two tokens

**Source.** Issue **#4206**, open, 4 comments, created **2026-10-26T10:23:33Z**. Box: 2x Radeon Pro VII 16 GB
(gfx906), 64 GB RAM, engine **0.1.50**, Qwen3.8 IQ3_S, layer split K=12, MTP on. A client that **appends** a
turn to a byte-identical prefix still re-reads the whole prompt:

```
conversation cache: parked 12750 tokens
prompt 12747 tokens = 0 reused + 12747 read in 25838 ms (493.3 tok/s), 8 generated in 158 ms, 1 checkpoints
```

Two mechanisms, both read from source with line numbers: (1) the checkpoint is only created inside the chunk
callback, where `done` is the **end of a whole chunk**, so the effective spacing is `max(N, chunk)` - *"setting
`--prompt-cache-every 4096` changes nothing at all"*; (2) the single end checkpoint is then rejected.
Measured, four configurations, two runs each:

| config | reused on the third request | wall |
|---|---|---|
| `--prefill auto:16384` (default) + `--prompt-cache-spread` | 0 | 25.19 s |
| same + `--prompt-cache-every 4096` | 0 | 25.18 s |
| `--prefill auto:4096` | 8192 | 12.09 s |
| `--prefill auto:8192` | 8192 | 12.26 s |

Cost of the smaller chunk on a cold 24k prompt: **599.3/599.0** tok/s at 16384, **567.6/587.2** at 8192,
**559.9/588.3** at 4096 - *"3-4 % prefill throughput"* for a **2x wall-time** win on an appended turn. Decode
and cache hit unchanged (50.5-50.8 tok/s, 0.985-0.988).

The third comment (**11:59:29Z**) walks back its own two earlier hypotheses and finds the real cause by
instrumenting `conversation_prefix()`: the checkpoint at `turn_at` covers history **up to** the last
`<|im_start|>`, so it also holds the previous `<|im_end|>` + `\n`, and a client that appends to the last
message puts its own content exactly where those two tokens were - six tokens match, the last two do not, so
the deepest checkpoint is unusable and reuse falls back to the previous chunk boundary. *"The missing
checkpoint is at a semantic position, not at a multiple of N, so density and granularity are two different
knobs here."* A local `STRATA_CKPT_BODY_AT=k` patch (k=2 for this template) fixes it.

**Applies:** directly to any agent workload - and it inverts the vendor's prompt-chunk advice for this
particular case: on our card the post-bump default **4096** gives *more* checkpoints than 8192, so the
`-9 % cold 8K prefill` the vendor prices may be partly bought back on appended turns. **Instrument:** our
protocol already distinguishes first-probe prefill from a warmed row (2026-10-20); this adds the third case -
an **appended** turn - and the number to read is `reused` and `N checkpoints` on the prompt line. **Cost:** one
engine start per chunk setting on a bumped engine.

### 10.1 [ARX] 2610.32009 "GS-MoE: Training-Free MoE Compression via Groupwise Subspace Sharing and Pruning"

Carson Cary, Yukai Jin, Yihao Huang, Zhankai Ye (2026-10-23T00:16:36Z). *"Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models
reduce computation through sparse activation, but **storing their expert parameters remains a major memory
bottleneck**. We introduce GS-MoE, a training-free compression framework that addresses redundancy across and
within experts through two compression axes: shared-subspace rank and expert intermediate width… Nyström-based
neuron compression reduces intermediate width within each expert… we derive per-expert reconstruction-error
reductions from the reconstruction objective and use them to guide expert grouping."*

**Why it matters here:** this lab's decode is bandwidth-bound on **expert bytes per token** - the 2026-10-20
bandwidth model (0.527 GiB of expert bytes per token for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) predicted our measured 47.4 and
83.0 tok/s from one constant. Anything that shrinks **per-expert intermediate width** attacks that constant
directly, and a **training-free** method is the only kind we could apply to a downloaded pack. Not testable
here: it needs a re-packed model, which is a build plus a pack, not a scan's call.

### 10.2 [ARX] 2610.32127 "MPQ: A Message-Passing View of Post-Training Quantization"

Yanshuo Chen, Chenxi Liu, Reza Shirkavand, Heng Huang (2026-10-23T03:54:41Z). *"Existing PTQ solvers,
including GPTQ, LDLQ, and block coordinate descent, commit each weight block to a single codeword before its
neighbors are considered, leaving near-optimal alternatives unexplored… we cast row-wise PTQ as posterior
inference over discrete codebooks, in which existing solvers correspond to a zero-temperature, hard-decision
limit… MPQ preserves soft posterior information across blocks during inference and commits to hard codewords
only at the output stage."*

**Why it matters here:** it is a **recipe change for the tool that produced our packs**. Our staged Qwen3.6
Q4_K_M and the ISTA RCO packs are all products of a quantiser; if the family of solvers behind them is a
zero-temperature special case, then a "better pack at the same size" is a real category rather than a hope.
Nothing to run: it needs a source model and a GPU-hour budget.

### 10.3 [ARX] 2610.32483 "When Agents Fail: Model or Harness - Who's to Blame?"

Shao Zhang, Hangfan Zhang, Yuanyuan Shi, Hao Li (2026-10-23T10:57:54Z). *"LLM agents combine models with
execution harnesses, so agentic benchmark performance reflects their joint behavior. We argue that harnesses
should therefore be evaluated through their interactions with underlying models. We develop a framework
combining five complementary measures: (i) **Valid Completion Rate (VCR)**, how reliably agents finish
normally and deliver evaluable results, **irrespective of task correctness**; (ii) **Model-Harness Fit**, how

**Why it matters here:** this is the paper form of three of our own instruments. **VCR** is exactly our
